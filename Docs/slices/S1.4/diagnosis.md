# S1.4 diagnosis: looking after somebody

Written by hand from `diagnosis-before.md`, generated by `S14DiagnosisTests` against the
S1.3 code with nothing changed. Shipped rules, the scenario as written, five design
conditions, seeds 1 to 10.

## 1. How the want is generated

One rule, `somebody_in_front_of_you_is_not_all_right`. It is raised separately for each
person in the room, and it is the sum of:

| Term | Factor | What it reads |
|---|---|---|
| memory of concern happening to them | 0.7 | the strongest reading of concern about that person today, salience x 20/(20 + minutes since) |
| memory of threat happening to them | 0.5 | the same, for threat |
| trait empathetic | 0.15 | who you are |
| value closeness | 0.2 | who you are |
| ledger: comforted me | 0.15 | the backstory, fixed |
| ledger: protected me | 0.15 | the backstory, fixed |

**Where the concern memory comes from.** The morning announces distress when it *starts*
to show (`show_distress`). Everybody who sees that reads it as concern about that
person, and that memory is what the want reads.

Across 10,211 times the want was raised, its urgency came from:
- traits and values: 49%
- memory: 48%
- ledger: 3%

**26% of the time there was no memory behind it at all**: it was raised on traits, values
and the backstory alone.

## 2. What serves it

- `sit_with_them` (comfort 0.9), `see_how_they_are` (watch 0.35), and
  `go_to_where_they_will_be` (walk to a common room 0.2).
- Sitting with somebody is also credited by keeping the peace (`settle_them_down` 0.4).
- There were 492 sittings in 50 mornings. Their appeal was 73% looking after and 27%
  keeping the peace.

## 3. What sitting with somebody does

When it finishes, the world does two things:

1. **It makes a `comfort` event,** valence good. Everybody who perceives it reads it as
   support: the one who sat (492), the one sat with (423), others in the room (802).
   Nobody outside the room or earshot kept a memory of it.
2. **It softens the other person's fear and anxiety to 0.55 of what they were,**
   directly (`Soothe`). That person perceives and appraises nothing. It is the S1.1
   technical debt.

## 4. Why nothing about it reaches the want

S1.2 found this want is never satisfied by acting on it. The measurements say why:

- **The want changes only as memories fade.** From choosing to sit to the next decision
  with the person still there, it fell 0.074 on average. Memory terms account for −0.076
  (time) and traits, values and ledger for 0.000. **The want could be walked back to the
  sitting in 0 of 479 cases.**
- **Nothing the helper can perceive changes because they helped.** Distress starting is an
  event; distress stopping is not. It stops 5.7 times a morning, 4.9 times with
  somebody in the room, and 0.8 times in the minute somebody finished sitting with that
  person. None of it can be perceived. So the one who sat cannot tell whether it
  worked, and neither can anybody else.
- **The memory the want reads cannot be answered.** It reads the strongest concern about
  that person, fading by the minute. Even if the end of the distress were perceived,
  nothing in the want would read it.
- **Most sittings have nothing visible to help.** The other person's distress was showing
  when the sitting finished in only 67 of 492 cases.
- **Help cannot be refused, and the world does not check that it happened.** In 69 of 492
  sittings the other person had left the room before it finished, and they were soothed
  anyway, wherever they were.
- **Being sat with can renew the want it served.** Distress shown again as a fresh event
  within fifteen minutes of being sat with happened 65 times. Softening somebody below
  the line and then watching them cross it again announces their distress a second time.

## 5. What could legitimately change it

| Candidate | Can it change because of what the helper did? |
|---|---|
| Traits, values | No. They are who the person is. |
| Ledger (comforted me, protected me) | No. Authored backstory, fixed; there is no live ledger. |
| Beliefs | None exist about how somebody else is. |
| Memory of concern about them | Only by fading, or by a fresh sight of distress. **Nothing that happens can answer it.** |
| The S1.3 outcome record | Not as built. It judges an act only against wants that read a need; this want reads none. Outcomes recorded for it: 0. |

## 6. The smallest missing causal link

The want reads **having seen the person go to pieces**. For what the helper does to change
it, the helper must be able to **see them come back together**, and the want must be
able to read that. Both halves are missing:

1. **A perception.** Distress stopping is as visible as distress starting, and the world
   does not announce it.
2. **A reading of it.** A memory of seeing somebody go to pieces presses until it fades,
   whatever is seen afterwards.

A third gap belongs to the attempt itself: **help that never reached the person is
treated as having happened.**

The S1.3 outcome record is not the carrier here. For hunger, what the want reads is the
body, and eating changes the body; the record only said why. For this want, what it reads
is a memory, so the only causal route from the act to the want is through what is
perceived and remembered.

**What the diagnosis does not support:**
- a satisfaction number;
- a record that lowers the want by itself;
- anything that fades faster after help.

**What it does support:**
- make the end of visible distress perceivable, by the same route as its start;
- let a memory of distress stop pressing once a later memory shows the same person come
  right;
- let the world refuse to resolve help that could not reach its person.
