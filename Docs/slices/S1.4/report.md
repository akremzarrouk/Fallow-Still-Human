**In short.** The social half of the lifecycle works. What actually happens to the person
reaches the helper's want, through what the helper can see and remember. It is traced link
by link, it is local, and it changes when the consequence is removed. It also changes the
house. With the concern answered, what remains of wanting to look after somebody is
who the helper is, and that is not enough to keep Elena from standing still like Leo.
Pacing comes back through S1.3's priced-out walks to the food. Two existing tests fail
because of it. Neither was tuned.

## 1. Diagnosis before the change

Full version in `diagnosis.md`, from `diagnosis-before.md`. In short:

1. **The want reads a memory, not a need.** `look_after` for each person in the room is
   the strongest memory today of seeing them go to pieces (concern 0.7, threat 0.5, each
   faded by 20/(20 + minutes)), plus the helper's empathy, closeness and two backstory
   ledger entries. Across 10,211 raisings its urgency came 48 % from memory, 49 % from
   traits and values, and 3 % from the ledger. 26 % of the time there was no memory
   behind it at all.
2. **Nothing the helper did reached it.** From choosing to sit to the next decision with
   the person there, the want fell 0.074, and memory fading accounts for all of it
   (−0.076). It could be walked back to the sitting in 0 of 479 cases.
3. **Nothing the helper could perceive changed because they helped.** Distress
   starting was an event. Distress stopping was not, though it stopped 5.7 times a
   morning. Nobody could see that somebody had come right, including whoever had just
   sat with them.
4. **The memory could not be answered.** Even a perceived recovery would not have
   entered the want: a memory pressed until it faded, whatever was seen afterwards.
5. **Help that never reached its person was treated as having happened.** In 69 of 492
   sittings the other person had left the room before it finished, and was soothed
   wherever they were.
6. **The S1.3 outcome record did not apply.** It judges an act against wants that read a
   need, and this want reads none: 0 outcomes. For hunger the want reads the body, and
   eating changes the body. For this want the only causal route from the act back to
   the want runs through perception and memory.

**Smallest missing link:** a perception of the person coming back together, and a want
that can read it. Plus one gap in the attempt itself: the world must check that the help
could reach its person.

## 2. Hypothesis

Committed before any code (`held-out-predictions.md`): *when somebody acts because they
want to look after another person, what actually happens to that person, as far as the
helper can perceive it, changes what the want reads.*

Planned change, from the diagnosis, and the only change made:
1. Distress stopping is perceivable, by the same route as distress starting, and only by
   people in the room.
2. A memory of seeing somebody go to pieces stops pressing once there is a later memory,
   about the same person, of seeing them come right. No number decides how much: it
   presses or it does not.
3. Sitting with somebody who has left the room before it finishes does nothing to them.

## 3. Exact changes

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `Scripts/Core/Sim/SilentMorning.cs` | **Distress stopping is an event.** When a person's visible distress stops and anybody is in the room, a `steady` event is made ("X seems to be holding together again"). It is seen, not heard (`audible: false`). If they were sat with that minute, the event rests on the settling record and on their own reading of being sat with. **Sitting with somebody checks they are still there.** If not, nothing happens to them, and a consequence record ("could not sit with X: they had gone") rests on the decision. `Soothe` is unchanged in effect; its record now rests on the decision that chose the sitting. |
| `Scripts/Core/Sim/Simulation.cs` | `Apply(event, causes)`: an event record may rest on what in the world caused it. |
| `Scripts/Core/Rules/Scaler.cs` | A memory scaler may name readings that answer it (`Until`). `ScalerTerm.Answered` marks a term that is zero because it was answered. |
| `Scripts/Core/Rules/ScalerEval.cs` | Recall skips a memory if a later memory of the same subject (same day, topic, person, actor) carries an answering reading. If every candidate was answered, the term is 0, marked answered, rests on the answering memory, and says so ("answered: reassurance at minute 28"). |
| `Scripts/Core/Sim/Motivator.cs` | A want rests on the records behind answered terms as well as non-zero ones, so a want that fell because it was answered walks back to the answer. |
| `Scripts/Core/Data/RuleSetLoader.cs`, `DecisionRuleValidator.cs` | Load `until`; reject an answer that is not a reading. |
| `Data/Rules/vocabulary.json` | Action `steady`; reading `reassurance`. |
| `Data/Rules/rules.json` | One interpretation rule: seeing `steady` is read as `reassurance` (weight 1.0, the only reading it can have). No appraisal rule, so no feeling follows. |
| `Data/Rules/decisions.json` | The two memory terms of `somebody_in_front_of_you_is_not_all_right` gain `"until": ["reassurance"]`. No factor, cost, proposal or other rule changed. |

**Not changed:** `Deliberator.cs`, standing still, every proposal and cost, every number

## 4. Experiments and results

Shipped rules, five design conditions, seeds 1 to 10 unless stated. Every table is
generated; see `experiment-results.md`.

### A, B, C, D controlled: the same moment ended four ways

Twelve real moments: somebody in the last minute of sitting with a person whose distress
is showing, having seen that person go to pieces. From the identical state:

| Ending | Cases | Helper saw them come right | Concern answered at the helper's next decision with them | Want rests on that sight | Memory part of the want then | Sat with them again within 20 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** as it happens | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 0.000 | 4 |
| **B** partial: shame being sat with does not settle (intervention) | 12 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.425 | 5 |
| **C** blocked: they had left (intervention) | 12 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0.278 | 5 |
| **D** no interaction: the helper had left (intervention) | 12 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.421 | 9 |

The one C case that was answered: the helper later saw the person come right on their
own, and the want rests on that. No concern was answered without the helper seeing the
person come right, in any ending. C's lower memory part is time: its next decision with
the person came 13.6 minutes later on average, against 0.0 for A and B.

### Natural: how the 296 sittings in 50 mornings ended

| How it ended | Sittings | Concern answered at the next decision | Memory part of the want, change | Further sittings with them within 20 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| They came right | 71 | 71 of 71 | −0.587 | 0.20 |
| Still visibly not all right | **0** | | | |
| They had gone | 45 | 8 of 40 (from other sights) | −0.214 | 0.09 |
| Nothing was showing | 180 | 158 of 180 (from earlier sights) | −0.155 | 0.29 |

**Partial help never happens on its own.** Being sat with is perceived by the person as
support and felt as gratitude. In every case that became a stronger feeling than whatever
distress was showing, so it stopped showing. The B row above exists only by intervention.

**What makes somebody come right when sat with** is mostly their own reading of it, not
the S1.1 `Soothe` shortcut. With the settling removed, on the same seeds, 76 sittings ended
with the person coming right, against 71 with it.

### C: blocked

`daniel_ate_it` seed 1, Elena sitting with Daniel. Moved out in the last minute: no
sitting happens, and Daniel's fear goes 0.464 → 0.624 over the same minutes. Left where he
was: sat with, and his fear goes 0.464 → 0.233. On the S1.3 code, 69 of 492 sittings soothed
somebody who had gone. After the change, none do.

### D: no interaction

On 12 moments where the helper sat and saw the person come right, the same person on the
same seed was left without the sitting. The concern at the helper's next decision with them
was 0.253 to 0.595, unanswered, every time.

## 10. What was actually demonstrated

- **A social want is causally connected to an action, and what the action actually did to
  the other person reaches that want.** 71 of 71 loops, from the sight of distress to the
  changed want, every link a real parent.
- **The route is perception and memory, not a score.**
  - The consequence is something the helper sees: the person coming back together, which
    rests on the person's own reading of being sat with.
  - What it changes is whether a memory still presses. The want's standing part (who
    the helper is) is untouched.
- **Removing the consequence removes the effect**, and the first difference is where it
  should be: in the person sat with when the settling is removed, and in the one who saw
  them come right when the sight is not read.
- **Success, partial help, blocked help and no help are told apart by what happened.** In
  the helper's want, only success answers the concern. The other three leave it standing,
  and differ in the record: a sitting with no recovery seen, a consequence saying they had
  gone, or no sitting at all.
- **Local:** only people in the room can see it, and only their own sight answers them.
- **Help that could not reach its person no longer happens.**
- **The S1.3 outcome record is not the carrier for this want.** The S1.3 principle is: a
  want reads state the consequence changes, and nothing decays it. That principle
  carries over. The record does not: it would have been a number standing in for a
  sight.

**Not demonstrated:**
- **Partial help, in the scenario as written.** It happens only by intervention.
- **That partial, blocked and absent help lead to different behaviour.** They all leave the
  concern standing. The small differences in resitting (5, 5, 9 of 12) are not shown to be
  caused.
- **That the change leaves the rest of the house alone.** It does not (section 9).

## 11. What was assumed

- **Visible distress stopping is what a helper can see of help working.** That is the
  existing rule for distress starting (dominant feeling among the distress feelings,
  times expressiveness, at least 0.30), turned round. Nothing about how the person feels
  inside is seen.
- **Seeing somebody come right answers having seen them go to pieces completely**, whoever
  or whatever brought it about, until they are seen to go to pieces again (a later concern
  memory is not answered by an earlier sight).
- **The answer is judged by reading, not by feeling.** `reassurance` has no appraisal rule.
  Whether seeing somebody come right should feel like relief was left alone.
- **"Later" means later in the order memories were made, on the same day, about the same
  person.**
- **The person's reading of being sat with (support, felt as gratitude) is existing S0/S1
  behaviour** and was not examined. It is why partial help never occurs.
- **`Soothe` remains technical debt.** It was not expanded; its record gained a parent.
- **Interventions:** shame 0.95 added to the person being sat with (B); moving one of the two
  into another room in the last minute (C, D). They are circumstances, not rules.

## 13. Recommendation

**MODIFY.**

Keep the three changes: the sight of somebody coming right, the `until` reading, and the
presence check. They are small (130 added lines of core code, many of them comments; one
rule; two vocabulary words). They are causal, local and traceable, and the architecture took them without a new
abstraction. Not REBUILD, not ABANDON.

But the slice cannot be called done while criterion 8 fails. What failed is not the
mechanism. It is what is left of the want once the mechanism works. `look_after` is now
raised with no circumstance behind it 83 % of the time, from traits and values alone. It
rarely wins, and the person falls through to standing still, or to S1.3's priced-out walks
to the food. The difference between Elena and Leo, and much of Mara's, lived in a concern
nothing could answer. S1.4 made that visible and did not fix it. The brief forbids fixing
it here, and it is not a number to tune.

| Component | Decision |
|---|---|
| `steady` sight, `reassurance` reading, `until` on memory terms | KEEP |
| Presence check on sitting with somebody | KEEP |
| S1.3 `PursuitOutcome` as the carrier for wants that read memory | ABANDON for this kind of want (keep it for needs) |
| The slice's effect on the house | MODIFY: the regression must be answered before building further on social wants |

## 14. Recommended next experiment

**What a want is when nothing in the circumstances calls for it.** After S1.4, most of
`look_after`, and by S1.2's audit about half of all urgency, is raised every decision by
who the person is, with nothing happening. Test, by diagnosis first, whether a want with no
circumstance behind it should be raised at all, or should only weigh how strongly a person
responds when a circumstance does arise. Measure what that does to Elena and Leo, to
standing still, and to the pacing loop.

Do this before a second social want (keeping the peace). Every social want has the same
standing part, and S1.4 shows that answering the circumstance leaves only that part
behind.

Second, if the first does not remove the pacing: S1.3's means-and-end question. Should a
walk to the food keep being valued by a want the person has already declined, at that
price, on arriving?
