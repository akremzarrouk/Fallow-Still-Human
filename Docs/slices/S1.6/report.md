# Slice S1.6 report: what a walk given up should change, and where

Date: 2026-09-16. Status: complete, stopped for review. Two switchable defect fixes, measured
alone and together on B; the shipped rules are unchanged and do not set either.
Reproduce with `./run-tests.sh`. **Full-suite tally pending at commit time**: the mechanism,
experiment and held-out fixtures (`S16MeansTests`, `S16ExperimentTests`, `S16HeldOutTests`)
were each run individually and pass exactly as reported below; the full suite, which also
confirms every S1–S1.5 generated file regenerates byte-identical, was still running when this
was committed and will be confirmed in a follow-up commit.

**In short.** The pacing S1.5 exposed under B is not a failure of feedback from acting into
memory, belief or motivation. Nothing is learned on a walk that is given up, and nothing could
be: 92.5 % of those walks were foreseeably pointless from what the walker already knew, and
every other one had somebody in the room on arrival. The want is right to persist. Two
pre-existing defects meet: deliberation credits a walk to a want without asking what could
be done at its end, and four scripted memories of the lived day carry no minute and never
age. Fixing the first (M2, `means: end`) removes every foreseeably pointless walk and takes
mean pacing from 2.74 to 0.07 with no new number; fixing the second (M1, a minute on the
event) lets `find_out` fade and be reactivated as designed. Together they leave a morning
in which walks are for something, what happens to a person stands out from noise in all
four conditions, and nobody wants nothing. They also leave three of the four people standing
still most of the morning, and Leo and Mara doing the same things: the differentiation A
had was standing wants, and the differentiation B had was the loop. What is left is what a
silent morning with one event in it can causally support. That is the finding to move on
with.

## 1. What was actually demonstrated

- **A walk given up on arrival teaches nothing, and could not.** Of 759 walks given up under B
  as not worth what it costs there, 702 (92.5 %) were foreseeably not worth taking at the
  moment of departure, weighed exactly as the deliberator weighs them on arrival, on the
  destination as the walker could imagine it. All 57 others had somebody in the room on
  arrival. Of 289 intentions that held, 286 were foreseen worth doing. On such a walk the
  walker gains two neutral experiences of leaving and arriving, 3 beliefs moved in 759 walks,
  and the want is the same or higher (`diagnosis-before.md` §2, §4).
- **The same defect is in A.** 144 of A's 439 walks (32.8 %) had ends foreseeably not worth
  doing; A's standing wants merely kept people from setting out. Every one of A's 185 walks
  to the food was given up on arrival.
- **Four scripted memories never fade.** `p01`–`p03` and the opening count carry minute −1,
  and recall treats no minute as fully fresh. For Daniel, Leo and Mara every `find_out` raised
  in 50 mornings rests on one of them at freshness 1.000 at the end of the morning. Elena,
  the actor of two of them, keeps them as her own intentions and her `find_out` rests on what
  she sees this morning.
- **M2 does what it says, with no number.** Under `end`, 0 of 474 walks (design) had ends
  foreseeably not worth doing (B: 811 of 1,218). A walk that is credited is worth exactly what
  it was (bench, to 1e-9). Mean pacing 2.74 → 0.07, worst 15 → 2, walks followed by something
  the walk was not for 72.4 % → 25.3 %, and every one of the 108 walks still given up had
  somebody in the room. The mechanism and the diagnosis instrument agree on every walk taken.
- **M1 does what it says.** Under `timed`, Leo's mean `find_out` falls from 0.38 in the first
  ten minutes to 0.09 in the last ten; Mara's 0.34 → 0.10; Daniel's 0.83 → 0.53, held up by the
  authored belief that he leads. M1 alone brings B's pacing to A's level (0.45) and halves the
  walks to the food (454 → 180), which was not predicted.
- **Together, B's causal properties survive:** 0 wants on traits and values alone, 95.9 %
  resting on something that happened, and the night stands out above noise in 4 of 4 design
  conditions (A: 2 of 4; B: 3 of 4), Daniel's now 2.6 × noise where A had 0.7. On the
  held-out case Leo's night is above the noise too (1.8), though less so than under A (2.7).
- **Nobody ever wants nothing** as written (0 of 3,689 decisions under both). With nothing
  missing, 373 decisions are led by no want; all 373 stand still. No idle motivation is needed.
- **The price is differentiation.** Mean different-person gap A 0.526, B 0.405, B with both
  0.267; closest pair 0.012 (Leo and Mara on `daniel_ate_it`). Standing still is 59.4 % of
  choices, more than under A, and now led by a want carried by a belief.

What was **not** demonstrated:
- That standing still 59 % of a silent morning is wrong. It is what these people, with these
  wants and these prices, have to do; the trace says why at every decision.
- That the residue of walks given up (somebody there) should be reduced. It is honest;
  reducing it needs the person to know where people were last seen, which no stage holds.
- Anything about a want that can be answered. Nothing in this slice can answer the missing
  can, because nobody speaks.

## 2. What was assumed

- **What a walker can foresee.** The destination is imagined from what they know: the room
  and whose it is (the house is not privileged), their own searches, their own look in the
  pantry, their hunger, their mind as it stands. Two things they cannot know are taken at
  their most favourable for the want: nobody is there unless the want is about somebody, in
  which case that person is; food kept there is within reach. So a walk is refused only when
  its end would not be worth doing even at best, and a walk taken can still disappoint.
- **Only walks are means**, as S1.2 decided. Foresight is one step: the walk and what could
  be done at its end. Nothing is sequenced.
- **The arrival judgement is the right question.** M2 asks before departure exactly what the
  deliberator asks on arrival (S1.2): of what serves the want here, is the best worth more
  than nothing, weighed with every want the person has and priced as this person prices it.
- **Minute 0 for the four scripted events.** They happen before the morning's first decision;
  the clock cannot say earlier. A memory with no minute stays fully fresh, which is right for
  days with no clock. In the experiment they are re-stamped in code; the loader now accepts
  `minute` so that the fix can be data.
- **The instruments.** S1.5's (audit seeds 1–10, batch seeds 1–40, the S1.1 noise measure,
  the sources instrument) and the S1.6 foresight instrument, which imagines the destination
  the same way M2 does and weighs it with the deliberator itself.
- **Held-out:** `leo_ate_it`, seeds 141–180, never run before.

## 5. What changed behaviour, and why

**Leo, `daniel_ate_it` seed 9.** Under B he alternates kitchen and back room from minute 25
to 89. Under `end`, every walk to the back room is weighed by its end before it is credited
(at minute 17: `go_to->back_room for find_out: search_room -0.519, not worth walking for`),
so from minute 25 he stands still for the supplies (`wait for guard_supplies`, 0.13 against
nothing else worth more than nothing) to the end. Under `timed` alone his
`find_out` is 0.20 by minute 25 and the walk is worth less than standing still; the same
morning results. The two fixes end the loop by different routes, and both routes are in the
trace (`experiment-results.md` §4, §7).

**Why M1 alone halves the walks to the food.** With `find_out` fading, people stop leaving
the kitchen to look for something, so they are no longer elsewhere with a reason to walk back
to the food. The walks to the food were mostly return legs of the loop. The prediction that
they would stay put was wrong on the design seeds and is checked on the held-out ones (P5).

**Why nobody eats under the fixes, where B had 20 meals.** B's meals were a by-product of
the pacing: the loop emptied the kitchen often enough for Daniel to be alone there with food
at a price he would pay. With people standing still where they are, which is mostly the
kitchen, eating in front of others stays priced out. With plenty and a count that is not
short, meals go on (48–54 in 50 mornings, Daniel and a few of Mara's). S1.3's account stands:
price, not the lifecycle.

**Why the walks that remain are given up.** Every one, under both `end` conditions, is a walk
to the food by somebody for whom eating was foreseen worth it alone, who arrived to find
somebody there. That is honest: the walker had no way to know. It is also the exact residue
that "where did I last see people" would remove, and nothing in the architecture holds that.

**Why people become alike.** Under A, Leo stood still for a standing wish to keep the peace
and Mara sat with people for a standing wish to look after them (S1.5). Under B those wants
need anxiety and seen distress; without them, both stand still for the supplies, sit with
whoever is upset, search the kitchen once, and look in the pantry once. On `daniel_ate_it`
their circumstances are the same: both innocent, both saw the count, both in the kitchen.
The model says temperament is how strongly you respond, and when every response to the same
circumstance is priced out or done, the strength does not show. Daniel differs because of an
authored belief (he leads, so he searches other people's rooms: `search_room +0.249`), and
the culprit differs because of the night (4 of 4 above noise). What differs is what
happened to them, which is what the architecture set out to make true.

**Standing still.** 59.4 % of choices, led by `guard_supplies` (a belief evidenced by the
count) in nearly every case, with `keep_peace` decisive in 23.6 %. It is not the unearned
credit S1.2 found: nothing credits standing still for what it avoids except `leave_it_alone`,
which is a want carried by a belief about the shelf. It is a house in which, after the first
twenty minutes, nothing worth doing is left for most people. That is a statement about the
scenario, and the trace can show it at every decision.

## 6. The question: where does the missing link belong?

**When a motivation causes an action, and the action shows the goal satisfied, impossible,
unnecessary, mistaken or changed, how should that feed back?** For this loop, it should not,
because the action showed none of those things. Walking to the back room did not make the
can less missing or Leo less unwilling to go through his mother's things. It showed him what
he could have computed: 702 of 759 times. The architecture already represents the correct
answer to "what did he learn?", which is nothing, and the correct answer to "does the want
still have a valid source?", which is yes.

The missing link is in **deliberation, at the moment a means is endorsed.** S1.2 made a walk
the one means in the slice and judged it on arrival; S1.6 makes the same judgement before
departure. That is not a plan and not a memory. It is the half of "a walk is for something"
that was not written down.

The second defect is in **the event record**: a scripted event of the lived day must say when
it happened, or its memory cannot age. Recall is right as it stands.

**What makes a remembered fact remain a current reason for action**, in this architecture,
is four things that all already exist: the pressing of the moment (fades over the recall
half life; broken only by the missing minute), reactivation (a fresher memory of the same
subject takes over: 174 `find_out` wants rest on a search seen this morning), answering
(`until`, S1.4; nothing here can answer the can), and durable belief (`supplies_short`
carries the shelf being short all morning, and moves by evidence). What does not exist is a
durable open question that could carry `find_out` unfading and be closed by an answer. It is
not what the loop needed, and building it now would recreate a want that never fades. It is
the right home for `find_out` once questions can be answered.

**Outcome feedback, the alternative,** was ruled out by measurement rather than argument:
if a lapse carried information the walker lacked, foresight could not predict it, and it
predicts 92.5 % with the rest accounted for by presence. A mechanism that learned from the
lapse would take every first pointless walk, would need a rule for when the lesson expires
that reduces to asking whether the end is worth it now, and would not explain the first walk.

**When nothing is wanted**, nothing is needed: 0 decisions as written; with nothing missing,
373 decisions led by no want, all standing still, the trace saying "nothing pressing". Noted
and not fixed: the band is absolute, so an act slightly not worth doing can tie with doing
nothing (96 of the 373 went to the seed, which chose standing still every time).

## 7. What failed

1. **Differentiation.** Mean gap 0.267 under both fixes (A 0.526, B 0.405), weakest condition
   0.229, closest pair 0.012. The fixes take away the loop, which S1.5 already said was part of
   what told people apart under B, and A's differentiation was standing wants, which S1.5
   proved wrong. What is left is small because the silent morning gives Leo and Mara the same
   circumstances. This is the honest number, and it will not be raised by anything in S1.
2. **Nobody eats as written**, under any fixed condition (B's 20 meals were the loop's).
3. **Walks given up because somebody is there** remain (75–108 in 50 mornings): the honest
   residue, needing knowledge of where people were last seen.
4. **Two design predictions were wrong:** M1 alone does not leave the walks to the food where
   they were (454 → 180), and M2 alone leaves standing still at 46 %, not "below A". Both are
   reported as they came out.
5. **The S1.4 regressions are not addressed**, as before: the pacing gate still fails on A,
   and the emergent-moment fading check.
6. **Five of ten held-out predictions missed** (section 9), all behavioural; every structural
   one held.

## 11. Recommendation

**MODIFY**, and then move on.

| Component | Decision |
|---|---|
| The foresight instrument and the walk records (`S16Measures`) | **KEEP** |
| M2, a walk credited only when its end could be worth doing (`means: end`) | **KEEP**, and ship: it completes S1.2's intention design, adds no number, and is traceable at every decision. |
| M1, scripted events of the lived day carry a minute | **KEEP**, and ship as data: `"minute": 0` on `p01`–`p03` and the opening. |
| Traits and values as standing wants (A) | **ABANDON**, as S1.5 said. Nothing that hid the loop is worth keeping. |
| B (`respond`) with M1 and M2 | **Ship as the S1 baseline.** S1.5 withheld B until the pacing had a cause-based answer; it has one. |
| A durable open question carrying `find_out` | **DEFER** to when a question can be answered. |
| Where people were last seen | **DEFER**; the residue it would remove is 75 walks in 50 mornings and honest. |

**Shipping is one data edit and three settings**, and it changes the regression baseline:
every generated file for S1 to S1.5 will change, and the tests that pin A's numbers
(`S15ExperimentTests.E2`, the S1.5 held-out misses, the S1.4 pacing gate, and every
characterisation test that asserts "nobody eats" or a count) must be turned round or
re-pinned deliberately, one by one, with the full suite run between. That is a review
decision and is left for it. The shipped rules in this commit set nothing.

**What this makes trustworthy enough to move on from:** under B with both fixes, every want
rests on something that happened or the body; every walk is for something the walker could
do; what happens to a person stands out above noise in every condition; and when nothing is
worth doing, people stand still for a reason the trace can name. What the silent morning
cannot supply is enough happening to tell two innocent people apart. The next experiment
should be the one the plan was heading for: something happens to a person during the
morning, and what they do later changes because of it, measured against the same person on
a morning where it did not. This slice makes that measurement clean: nothing standing and
nothing looping is left to dilute it.

Stopped here for review. No further S1 feature was started.
