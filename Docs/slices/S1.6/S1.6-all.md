## report.md

### Slice S1.6 report: what a walk given up should change, and where

Date: 2026-09-16. Status: complete, stopped for review. Two switchable defect fixes, measured
alone and together on B; the shipped rules are unchanged and do not set either.
Reproduce with `./run-tests.sh`. **[[SUITE]]**

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `diagnosis.md` | Why people walk to ends they will not pursue, in words (by hand) |
| `diagnosis-before.md` | Generated by `S16DiagnosisTests` on the S1.5 code, before any change (frozen, commit `eeb29fe`) |
| `diagnosis-current.md` | The same tests on the current code (the instrument is unchanged, so identical) |
| `held-out-predictions.md` | Hypothesis, the two mechanisms and predictions, committed before any code for them (`c57687a`) |
| `experiment-results.md` | The 2 × 2 on B, with A for reference, generated by `S16ExperimentTests` |
| `held-out-results.md` | The predictions checked on `leo_ate_it` seeds 141–180, generated by `S16HeldOutTests` |
| `S1.6-all.md` | All of the above merged into one document, by `merge-slice-docs.py` |

**In short.** The pacing S1.5 exposed under B is not a failure of feedback from acting into
memory, belief or motivation. Nothing is learned on a walk that is given up, and nothing could
be: 92.5 % of those walks were foreseeably pointless from what the walker already knew, and
every other one had somebody in the room on arrival. The want is right to persist. Two
pre-existing defects meet: deliberation credits a walk to a want without asking what could
be done at its end, and four scripted memories of the lived day carry no minute and never
age. Fixing the first (M2, `means: end`) removes every foreseeably pointless walk and takes
mean pacing from 2.74 to 0.07 with no new number; fixing the second (M1, a minute on the
event) lets `find_out` fade and be reactivated as designed. Together they leave a morning
in which walks are for something, what happens to a person stands out from noise in all
four conditions, and nobody wants nothing. They also leave three of the four people standing
still most of the morning, and Leo and Mara doing the same things: the differentiation A
had was standing wants, and the differentiation B had was the loop. What is left is what a
silent morning with one event in it can causally support. That is the finding to move on
with.

#### 1. What was actually demonstrated

- **A walk given up on arrival teaches nothing, and could not.** Of 759 walks given up under B
  as not worth what it costs there, 702 (92.5 %) were foreseeably not worth taking at the
  moment of departure, weighed exactly as the deliberator weighs them on arrival, on the
  destination as the walker could imagine it. All 57 others had somebody in the room on
  arrival. Of 289 intentions that held, 286 were foreseen worth doing. On such a walk the
  walker gains two neutral experiences of leaving and arriving, 3 beliefs moved in 759 walks,
  and the want is the same or higher (`diagnosis-before.md` §2, §4).
- **The same defect is in A.** 144 of A's 439 walks (32.8 %) had ends foreseeably not worth
  doing; A's standing wants merely kept people from setting out. Every one of A's 185 walks
  to the food was given up on arrival.
- **Four scripted memories never fade.** `p01`–`p03` and the opening count carry minute −1,
  and recall treats no minute as fully fresh. For Daniel, Leo and Mara every `find_out` raised
  in 50 mornings rests on one of them at freshness 1.000 at the end of the morning. Elena,
  the actor of two of them, keeps them as her own intentions and her `find_out` rests on what
  she sees this morning.
- **M2 does what it says, with no number.** Under `end`, 0 of 474 walks (design) had ends
  foreseeably not worth doing (B: 811 of 1,218). A walk that is credited is worth exactly what
  it was (bench, to 1e-9). Mean pacing 2.74 → 0.07, worst 15 → 2, walks followed by something
  the walk was not for 72.4 % → 25.3 %, and every one of the 108 walks still given up had
  somebody in the room. The mechanism and the diagnosis instrument agree on every walk taken.
- **M1 does what it says.** Under `timed`, Leo's mean `find_out` falls from 0.38 in the first
  ten minutes to 0.09 in the last ten; Mara's 0.34 → 0.10; Daniel's 0.83 → 0.53, held up by the
  authored belief that he leads. M1 alone brings B's pacing to A's level (0.45) and halves the
  walks to the food (454 → 180), which was not predicted.
- **Together, B's causal properties survive:** 0 wants on traits and values alone, 95.9 %
  resting on something that happened, and the night stands out above noise in 4 of 4 design
  conditions (A: 2 of 4; B: 3 of 4), Daniel's now 2.6 × noise where A had 0.7. On the
  held-out case Leo's night is above the noise too (1.8), though less so than under A (2.7).
- **Nobody ever wants nothing** as written (0 of 3,689 decisions under both). With nothing
  missing, 373 decisions are led by no want; all 373 stand still. No idle motivation is needed.
- **The price is differentiation.** Mean different-person gap A 0.526, B 0.405, B with both
  0.267; closest pair 0.012 (Leo and Mara on `daniel_ate_it`). Standing still is 59.4 % of
  choices, more than under A, and now led by a want carried by a belief.

What was **not** demonstrated:
- That standing still 59 % of a silent morning is wrong. It is what these people, with these
  wants and these prices, have to do; the trace says why at every decision.
- That the residue of walks given up (somebody there) should be reduced. It is honest;
  reducing it needs the person to know where people were last seen, which no stage holds.
- Anything about a want that can be answered. Nothing in this slice can answer the missing
  can, because nobody speaks.

#### 2. What was assumed

- **What a walker can foresee.** The destination is imagined from what they know: the room
  and whose it is (the house is not privileged), their own searches, their own look in the
  pantry, their hunger, their mind as it stands. Two things they cannot know are taken at
  their most favourable for the want: nobody is there unless the want is about somebody, in
  which case that person is; food kept there is within reach. So a walk is refused only when
  its end would not be worth doing even at best, and a walk taken can still disappoint.
- **Only walks are means**, as S1.2 decided. Foresight is one step: the walk and what could
  be done at its end. Nothing is sequenced.
- **The arrival judgement is the right question.** M2 asks before departure exactly what the
  deliberator asks on arrival (S1.2): of what serves the want here, is the best worth more
  than nothing, weighed with every want the person has and priced as this person prices it.
- **Minute 0 for the four scripted events.** They happen before the morning's first decision;
  the clock cannot say earlier. A memory with no minute stays fully fresh, which is right for
  days with no clock. In the experiment they are re-stamped in code; the loader now accepts
  `minute` so that the fix can be data.
- **The instruments.** S1.5's (audit seeds 1–10, batch seeds 1–40, the S1.1 noise measure,
  the sources instrument) and the S1.6 foresight instrument, which imagines the destination
  the same way M2 does and weighs it with the deliberator itself.
- **Held-out:** `leo_ate_it`, seeds 141–180, never run before.

#### 5. What changed behaviour, and why

**Leo, `daniel_ate_it` seed 9.** Under B he alternates kitchen and back room from minute 25
to 89. Under `end`, every walk to the back room is weighed by its end before it is credited
(at minute 17: `go_to->back_room for find_out: search_room -0.519, not worth walking for`),
so from minute 25 he stands still for the supplies (`wait for guard_supplies`, 0.13 against
nothing else worth more than nothing) to the end. Under `timed` alone his
`find_out` is 0.20 by minute 25 and the walk is worth less than standing still; the same
morning results. The two fixes end the loop by different routes, and both routes are in the
trace (`experiment-results.md` §4, §7).

**Why M1 alone halves the walks to the food.** With `find_out` fading, people stop leaving
the kitchen to look for something, so they are no longer elsewhere with a reason to walk back
to the food. The walks to the food were mostly return legs of the loop. The prediction that
they would stay put was wrong on the design seeds and is checked on the held-out ones (P5).

**Why nobody eats under the fixes, where B had 20 meals.** B's meals were a by-product of
the pacing: the loop emptied the kitchen often enough for Daniel to be alone there with food
at a price he would pay. With people standing still where they are, which is mostly the
kitchen, eating in front of others stays priced out. With plenty and a count that is not
short, meals go on (48–54 in 50 mornings, Daniel and a few of Mara's). S1.3's account stands:
price, not the lifecycle.

**Why the walks that remain are given up.** Every one, under both `end` conditions, is a walk
to the food by somebody for whom eating was foreseen worth it alone, who arrived to find
somebody there. That is honest: the walker had no way to know. It is also the exact residue
that "where did I last see people" would remove, and nothing in the architecture holds that.

**Why people become alike.** Under A, Leo stood still for a standing wish to keep the peace
and Mara sat with people for a standing wish to look after them (S1.5). Under B those wants
need anxiety and seen distress; without them, both stand still for the supplies, sit with
whoever is upset, search the kitchen once, and look in the pantry once. On `daniel_ate_it`
their circumstances are the same: both innocent, both saw the count, both in the kitchen.
The model says temperament is how strongly you respond, and when every response to the same
circumstance is priced out or done, the strength does not show. Daniel differs because of an
authored belief (he leads, so he searches other people's rooms: `search_room +0.249`), and
the culprit differs because of the night (4 of 4 above noise). What differs is what
happened to them, which is what the architecture set out to make true.

**Standing still.** 59.4 % of choices, led by `guard_supplies` (a belief evidenced by the
count) in nearly every case, with `keep_peace` decisive in 23.6 %. It is not the unearned
credit S1.2 found: nothing credits standing still for what it avoids except `leave_it_alone`,
which is a want carried by a belief about the shelf. It is a house in which, after the first
twenty minutes, nothing worth doing is left for most people. That is a statement about the
scenario, and the trace can show it at every decision.

#### 6. The question: where does the missing link belong?

**When a motivation causes an action, and the action shows the goal satisfied, impossible,
unnecessary, mistaken or changed, how should that feed back?** For this loop, it should not,
because the action showed none of those things. Walking to the back room did not make the
can less missing or Leo less unwilling to go through his mother's things. It showed him what
he could have computed: 702 of 759 times. The architecture already represents the correct
answer to "what did he learn?", which is nothing, and the correct answer to "does the want
still have a valid source?", which is yes.

The missing link is in **deliberation, at the moment a means is endorsed.** S1.2 made a walk
the one means in the slice and judged it on arrival; S1.6 makes the same judgement before
departure. That is not a plan and not a memory. It is the half of "a walk is for something"
that was not written down.

The second defect is in **the event record**: a scripted event of the lived day must say when
it happened, or its memory cannot age. Recall is right as it stands.

**What makes a remembered fact remain a current reason for action**, in this architecture,
is four things that all already exist: the pressing of the moment (fades over the recall
half life; broken only by the missing minute), reactivation (a fresher memory of the same
subject takes over: 174 `find_out` wants rest on a search seen this morning), answering
(`until`, S1.4; nothing here can answer the can), and durable belief (`supplies_short`
carries the shelf being short all morning, and moves by evidence). What does not exist is a
durable open question that could carry `find_out` unfading and be closed by an answer. It is
not what the loop needed, and building it now would recreate a want that never fades. It is
the right home for `find_out` once questions can be answered.

**Outcome feedback, the alternative,** was ruled out by measurement rather than argument:
if a lapse carried information the walker lacked, foresight could not predict it, and it
predicts 92.5 % with the rest accounted for by presence. A mechanism that learned from the
lapse would take every first pointless walk, would need a rule for when the lesson expires
that reduces to asking whether the end is worth it now, and would not explain the first walk.

**When nothing is wanted**, nothing is needed: 0 decisions as written; with nothing missing,
373 decisions led by no want, all standing still, the trace saying "nothing pressing". Noted
and not fixed: the band is absolute, so an act slightly not worth doing can tie with doing
nothing (96 of the 373 went to the seed, which chose standing still every time).

#### 7. What failed

1. **Differentiation.** Mean gap 0.267 under both fixes (A 0.526, B 0.405), weakest condition
   0.229, closest pair 0.012. The fixes take away the loop, which S1.5 already said was part of
   what told people apart under B, and A's differentiation was standing wants, which S1.5
   proved wrong. What is left is small because the silent morning gives Leo and Mara the same
   circumstances. This is the honest number, and it will not be raised by anything in S1.
2. **Nobody eats as written**, under any fixed condition (B's 20 meals were the loop's).
3. **Walks given up because somebody is there** remain (75–108 in 50 mornings): the honest
   residue, needing knowledge of where people were last seen.
4. **Two design predictions were wrong:** M1 alone does not leave the walks to the food where
   they were (454 → 180), and M2 alone leaves standing still at 46 %, not "below A". Both are
   reported as they came out.
5. **The S1.4 regressions are not addressed**, as before: the pacing gate still fails on A,
   and the emergent-moment fading check.
6. **Five of ten held-out predictions missed** (section 9), all behavioural; every structural
   one held.

**Shipping is one data edit and three settings**, and it changes the regression baseline:
every generated file for S1 to S1.5 will change, and the tests that pin A's numbers
(`S15ExperimentTests.E2`, the S1.5 held-out misses, the S1.4 pacing gate, and every
characterisation test that asserts "nobody eats" or a count) must be turned round or
re-pinned deliberately, one by one, with the full suite run between. That is a review
decision and is left for it. The shipped rules in this commit set nothing.

**What this makes trustworthy enough to move on from:** under B with both fixes, every want
rests on something that happened or the body; every walk is for something the walker could
do; what happens to a person stands out above noise in every condition; and when nothing is
worth doing, people stand still for a reason the trace can name. What the silent morning
cannot supply is enough happening to tell two innocent people apart. The next experiment
should be the one the plan was heading for: something happens to a person during the
morning, and what they do later changes because of it, measured against the same person on
a morning where it did not. This slice makes that measurement clean: nothing standing and
nothing looping is left to dilute it.

Stopped here for review. No further S1 feature was started.

## diagnosis.md

### S1.6 diagnosis: why people walk to ends they will not pursue

Written by hand from `diagnosis-before.md`, generated by `S16DiagnosisTests` on the S1.5 code
with nothing about deciding or memory changed. Measured on B (`respond`), where S1.5 found the
loop plain; the shipped rules are untouched. The five design conditions, seeds 1 to 10:
50 mornings, 3,961 decisions, 1,218 walks.

The question S1.5 left: when somebody walks somewhere for a want and, on arriving, finds the
end not worth pursuing, what should that change, and where in the pipeline does the change
belong? This diagnosis answers the second half first, because the traces settle it.

#### 1. The loop, mechanically

Leo, `daniel_ate_it` seed 9, minute 25, in the kitchen with Elena and Mara. He wants food
(0.55, hunger), to find out what happened to the can (0.46), the supplies to last (0.43) and the
house to stay quiet (0.18). He has already been through the kitchen and looked in the pantry.
What is left to him for `find_out` is to watch somebody (worth 0.21, costs him 0.28) or to walk
to the nearest private room he has not searched (worth 0.32, costs 0.18). He walks. It ties
with standing still (0.17 against 0.22, inside the band) and the seed settles it.

Minute 29, in the back room, alone, holding the intention to find out. The only thing there
that serves it is going through the room, worth 0.37 and costing him 0.72: it is Elena and
Mara's room, and he is empathetic and values their autonomy. The intention lapses as not worth
what it costs here. Weighed openly, walking back to the kitchen for food is worth 0.31 and
costs 0.18; it wins clearly. Minute 33, in the kitchen, holding the intention to eat: eating
costs him 1.42 against 0.58. Lapsed. Walking to the back room for `find_out` is worth 0.32
again. And so on, every four minutes, until minute 89.

Two things hold the loop up:

1. **The walk is worth what its want is worth, whatever waits at the end.** The proposal
   `somewhere_worth_looking` endorses the walk at 0.7 of the want; `go_where_the_food_is` at
   0.55. Neither asks what could be done on arrival. The end of each walk is priced out for
   Leo by his own values before he takes a step, and the walk is priced only as a walk.
2. **`find_out` never falls.** It is 0.46 at minute 1 and 0.46 at minute 89, for Leo, on every
   seed. Section 3.

#### 2. What a walk given up leaves behind, and what it could have told him

The brief asks what actually happened, what was perceived, what was learned, and what
proposition changed. On 759 walks given up as not worth it (`diagnosis-before.md` §4):

| | |
|---|---|
| What happened in the world | Leaving a room and entering one: two events, with witnesses only if somebody was there. Nothing else. |
| What the walker perceived | Their own leaving and arriving: 1,311 of 1,327 experiences neutral. |
| What they learned | 3 beliefs moved in 759 walks, none about the want. |
| What was recorded | An outcome for the 454 food walks (S1.3), which moves nothing by design. Nothing for the 305 `find_out` walks. |
| The want on arrival | The same as at departure in 304 (`find_out`), higher in 454 (hunger rose on the way), lower in 1. |

So nothing was learned, and nothing was there to learn. The decisive measurement is §2 of
`diagnosis-before.md`: **at the moment each walker set out, the best thing they could have
done for the walk's want on arrival, weighed exactly as the deliberator weighs it on arrival,
on the destination as they could imagine it from what they already knew, was not worth doing
in 702 of the 759 walks later given up (92.5 %).** All 57 exceptions are walks where somebody
turned out to be in the room, which raises the price of eating or of going through their
things; nobody could have known that. And of 289 intentions that held on arrival, 286 were
foreseen worth doing. The instrument and the deliberator agree on 988 of 1,048 arrivals.

The 2 ⁄ 3 of all walks that were pointless from the start go to three places: the kitchen for
food that eating is priced out of (417), and the back room (292) and the brothers' room (102)
to look for something in a room the walker will not go through.

**What this rules out.** A mechanism that feeds the lapse back into memory, belief or the
applicability of the want would be teaching the person by trial what they could already
compute. It would always take the first pointless walk. It would need a criterion for when the
lesson expires (hunger rises by 0.01 on every walk), and any honest criterion reduces to asking
whether the end is worth it now, which is the question that was never asked. And it would not
explain why the walk was taken in the first place. The question "what did the character learn
by going there?" has the answer "nothing", and the architecture already represents that
correctly: the want still has a valid source (the can is still missing; he is still hungry),
nothing removed it, and nothing should have.

**Where the missing link belongs.** Deliberation, at the moment a means is endorsed. The
architecture already treats a walk as the one means in the slice: an intention survives it and
is judged on arrival against what serves it (S1.2). The half that is missing is that the same
judgement is not made before setting out. A person does not walk to their mother's room in
order to not go through it.

#### 3. The memory that never fades, and what keeps a remembered fact a reason

`find_out` is raised on memories of today about the missing can (concern, threat). A memory
presses at salience × freshness, and freshness is 20 / (20 + minutes since it happened). Four
events of the lived day are scripted and carry no minute: the three day-4 events of the
backstory (`p01`–`p03`) and the opening count. Recall treats a memory with no minute as fully
fresh. `diagnosis-before.md` §1:

- For Daniel, Leo and Mara, **every** `find_out` raised in 50 mornings rests on one of these
  (976, 1,061 and 939 wants), at freshness 1.000 in the last ten minutes of the morning. Leo's
  rests on Elena's "nobody in this house would take food", which he read as a subject being
  closed; Daniel's and Mara's on the count.
- Elena's rests on nothing scripted: she is the actor of the count and of `p03`, so she keeps
  them as her own intentions, and her `find_out` (0.23–0.32, moving) comes from searches she
  watches this morning, read as threats. Hers is the want as designed.
- Reactivation already works: 174 wants rest on a search seen this morning rather than on the
  scripted memory, because a newer memory of the same subject pressed harder.

This is a defect in how time is represented for scripted events, not in recall. The designer
chose memory over feeling to carry `find_out` "because the feeling fades", meaning slower, not
never. It is what makes the `find_out` half of the loop last the whole morning, and it makes
`find_out` a standing want in all but name for three of the four, in A as much as in B.

**What makes a remembered fact remain a current reason for action?** In this architecture,
four things, all already present:

1. The pressing of the moment, which fades with the recall half life. (Broken only for
   scripted events, by the missing minute.)
2. Reactivation: a later event on the same subject that presses harder takes over (174 cases).
3. Answering: a later memory carrying a reading that closes the earlier one (`until`, S1.4).
   Nothing in this slice can answer the missing can, because nobody speaks.
4. Durable belief: the can being missing lives on in `supplies_short`, evidenced by the count,
   and carries `guard_supplies` all morning. It fades by evidence, not by time.

What is not represented is a durable open question ("what happened to it is unknown") that
could carry `find_out` without fading and be closed by an answer. That is the right home for
the want once questions can be answered, and it is not the cause of the loop: with the
timing defect fixed, the loop needs only the means-and-end failure to continue on the food
side, and with the means-and-end failure fixed, an unfading `find_out` would find nothing
affordable to do and stop. Adding it now would recreate a want that never fades.

#### 4. When nothing is wanted

`diagnosis-before.md` §5. Under B, in 3,961 decisions as written, nobody was ever without a
want: hunger and the supplies are always there. With nothing missing, 66 decisions had no
want behind what was chosen. All 66 stood still. In 42 of them the seed settled it, between
standing still at exactly nothing and sitting with somebody or walking somewhere at slightly
less than nothing. Standing still won every time it was chosen for nothing.

So no idle motivation is needed. Doing nothing at a score of zero, led by no want, is what
the architecture already does, and the trace says "nothing pressing". One thing to note and
not fix here: the ambiguity band is absolute, so an act that is slightly not worth doing can
tie with doing nothing and be picked by the coin.

#### 5. What the diagnosis supports

- The pacing is two pre-existing defects meeting, neither of which is feedback from acting:
  a walk is endorsed without regard to its end (deliberation), and four scripted memories of
  the lived day never age (time in the event record).
- Nothing is learned on a walk that is given up, and nothing could be: 92.5 % of lapses were
  foreseeable from what the walker knew, and the rest are somebody being there.
- The want is right to persist; the means was wrong to be proposed.
- Reactivation and answering already exist for memories. A durable carrier for an open
  question does not, and is not what the loop needs.
- Nothing is needed for the case of wanting nothing.

#### 6. What it does not show

- Whether pricing a walk by its end changes behaviour for the better, or merely removes
  walks: what people do instead is the experiment.
- Whether the timing fix on its own would end the loop (it should not: 454 walks to food are
  given up with hunger rising, and hunger is not scripted).
- What happens to the walks whose end depends on who is there. The instrument imagines the
  most favourable room; the 57 walks where somebody was present are the honest residue, and
  reducing them would need the person to know where people were last seen.

## held-out-predictions.md

### S1.6 hypothesis, the two mechanisms, and held-out predictions

Written and committed after the diagnosis and **before any code for either mechanism
exists**. Nothing below has been run.

#### Hypothesis

The pacing S1.5 exposed under B is not a failure of feedback from acting into memory, belief
or motivation. Nothing is learned on a walk that is given up, and nothing could be: 92.5 % of
those walks were foreseeably not worth taking from what the walker already knew, and the rest
are somebody being in the room. The want is right to persist. What is wrong is that a means
was proposed without regard to its end, and that half of the wants behind the walks rest on
scripted memories that never age.

So two changes, each a defect fix in its own stage, and each switchable so that it can be
measured alone and with the other:

#### M1: scripted events of the lived day carry the clock's minute

**Where:** the event record (world truth), and the data. An event may say when in the day it
happened. The four scripted events of day 4 (`p01`–`p03` in the backstory, the opening count)
happen before the morning's first decision, so they carry minute 0. Nothing about recall
changes: freshness is what it always was, 20 / (20 + minutes since). A memory that carries
no minute is still treated as fresh, which is right for days with no clock, and after this
no memory of the lived day is without one.

In the experiment the four events are re-stamped in code; the loader accepts `minute` so that
the fix can be data when it ships.

#### M2: a walk is endorsed for a want only if its end could be worth doing

**Where:** deliberation, at the moment a proposal endorses a walk. Before crediting a walk to
a want, the deliberator asks the question it already asks on arrival: of everything that could
be done for this want at the destination other than walking on, is the best worth more than
nothing, weighed with every want the person has now and priced as this person prices it? If
not, the walk is not endorsed for that want. The trace records what was foreseen either way.

The destination is imagined from what the walker knows and nothing else: the room and whose
it is (the house is not privileged), the rooms they have been through, whether they have
looked in the pantry, how hungry they are, and their own mind as it stands. Two things they
cannot know are taken at their most favourable for the want: nobody is there unless the want
is about somebody, in which case that person is; food kept there is within reach. If the end
is not worth it even then, the walk could not have been worth taking. If it is, the walk is
taken and the arrival may still disappoint, honestly, because of who turned out to be there
or what turned out to be gone.

**No number changes.** A walk that is endorsed is worth exactly what it was: urgency × fit.
Nothing is added, subtracted, decayed, or remembered. A walk endorsed for one want and not
for another keeps the first want's contribution only. The arrival judgement is unchanged.

Selected by `deciding.means`: `want` (the default, which the shipped rules keep: a walk is
worth its want) or `end` (M2). Removing the setting restores the shipped behaviour exactly.

#### What this is not

- Not a cooldown, a blacklist, a boredom, a penalty on walking, a value on standing still,
  or a memory of having been somewhere. A walk that is worth taking is taken every time it
  is worth taking, and one that is not is never taken, from the first.
- Not a plan. One step: the walk and what could be done at its end. Nothing is sequenced.
- Not a fix for a want: `find_out` under M1 fades and is reactivated by what happens; under
  M2 it stays whatever it is. Neither touches its sources.

#### Not changed

Perception, interpretation, appraisal, emotions, beliefs, the ledger, `PursuitOutcome`,
`until`, presence, interruption, food and eating, every proposal, every cost, every
motivation rule, every number in the rule files, the ambiguity band, and everything about
how traits and values enter a want (B stays `respond` throughout).

#### Conditions

On B (`respond`), a 2 × 2: M1 off/on × M2 off/on. Plus A (`standing`) as shipped, and A with
M1, to see what the timing fix does where standing wants hide the loop.

#### Measured, with the S1.5 instruments and the S1.6 diagnosis instrument

1. Walks set out on; walks whose end was foreseeably not worth doing; walks given up on
   arrival, and how many of those had somebody in the room.
2. Pacing, mean and worst; walks followed by something the walk's want did not serve.
3. `find_out` over the morning, by ten minutes, per person.
4. Standing still, share and on top; each want's decisive rate; sittings; searches.
5. Different-person gap: mean, weakest condition, closest pair.
6. The night against no night, over noise (S1.1 measure), for the four design culprits.
7. Meals, as written and with plenty.
8. Where wants come from (unsupported, traced), so that B's causal properties are seen to
   survive both changes.
9. Decisions led by no want, and what was chosen.
10. Leo on `daniel_ate_it` seed 9, minute by minute, under each condition.
11. One walk under M2 traced, showing what was foreseen and why a walk was or was not endorsed.

#### Predictions on the design conditions (seeds 1 to 10 and 1 to 40)

Stated so that they can be wrong.

- **M2 alone** removes the walks to ends that were foreseeably not worth it: 0 such walks
  set out on. Walks given up on arrival fall to under 10 % of walks that arrive, and every
  one has somebody in the room. Pacing falls below A's (0.43). `find_out` stays flat.
- **M1 alone** makes `find_out` fall over the morning for Daniel, Leo and Mara (the last ten
  minutes under half the first ten) and removes most walks to look for something after the
  first twenty minutes, but leaves the walks to the food (about 450 given up under B) nearly
  where they are, so pacing stays well above A's.
- **Both**: the least pacing of the four, and walks given up under 10 %.
- **A with M1** changes A's behaviour little: pacing stays near 0.43 and standing still near
  half of choices, because A's standing wants are what decide it.
- Under both, B's causal properties hold: nothing raised on traits and values alone, and
  wants traced at or above 90 %.

#### Held-out condition

`leo_ate_it`, **seeds 141 to 180**. No test has run those seeds.

#### Predictions for `leo_ate_it`, seeds 141 to 180
