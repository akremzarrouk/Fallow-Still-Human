# S1.7 diagnosis: what can carry a past event into a later decision

Written by hand from `diagnosis-before.md`, generated by `S17DiagnosisTests` on the shipped
rules with nothing changed. Everything below is measured by running the thing it describes,
not read off the source.

## 1. Only one thing crosses midnight

Three things could carry an earlier event into a later decision, and they have completely
different lifetimes.

| Carrier | Lives for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Memory** | the day it happened | `ScalerEval.Recall` filters `x.Day == ctx.Today`. A memory of an earlier day is kept forever and read by nothing that raises a want. |
| **Feeling** | about an hour | decays every fade; by sixteen fades nothing is left of six earlier events. |
| **Belief** | for ever | nothing anywhere decays one. Thirty days later it is exactly where it was. |

So a question about longitudinal causation in this architecture is, almost entirely, a
question about beliefs. The design brief's phrase "memory persistence" and its phrase "causal
relevance" turn out to name two different objects here, not two aspects of one.

## 2. The ledger is nearly write-only

Eight kinds of ledger entry exist. The scenario data writes five of them, fourteen times in
all, every one as an authored `ledger_effects` block on a hand-written event. **No rule
anywhere turns what an event meant into a ledger entry**, so the record of what people have
done to each other is authored rather than simulated. Three of the eight kinds are read by any
rule at all; `searched_my_things`, written into Mara's ledger about Daniel at weight 0.8 by the
existing `p02`, is read by nothing.

This is why the accusation-and-compliance scenario the brief suggested would not have measured
the simulation: the variable it rests on has no wire attached at either end.

## 3. Two kinds of belief, with different causal reach

A belief matters longitudinally only if experience can write it and something later reads it.
Both halves exist, and they come in two shapes.

| Belief | Written from experience by | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `tendency(x, does_not_respect_me)` | any `disrespect` reading (up), any `support` reading (down) | interpretation only |
| `tendency(x, treats_me_like_a_child)` | any `disrespect` reading addressed to me | interpretation only |
| `tendency(x, needs_to_be_in_charge)` | **nothing** | interpretation only |
| `answerable_for(me, topic)` | doing the thing, witnessed | interpretation only |
| `supplies_short` | counting a short shelf, watching someone eat | interpretation **and the `guard_supplies` want** |
| `role_claim(me, leads_family)` | **nothing** | interpretation **and the `find_out` want** |

A belief a **motivation** rule reads moves a want directly and by any amount. A belief only
**interpretation** reads has to go through a reading first, and section 4 is what that costs.

## 4. Nothing downstream of a reading can see history

Two independent facts, both measured:

- **No appraisal rule reads history.** All eighteen are weighed by traits, values and
  perceptiveness, and by nothing else. What an event feels like is decided by the reading it
  got, who you are, and who was watching.
- **A reading carries no strength with it.** `Simulation.Perceive` hands appraisal and the
  belief nudges the winning *meaning* and nothing else. The weight that reading won by is
  written to the trace and read by nothing.

Together these mean a belief about a person can reach a feeling **only by changing which
reading wins**. The measurement: the same person, the same later event, one earlier slight
against six. The belief differs (0.120 against 0.536), the reading is won by more (0.653
against 0.819), and every number after the reading is identical to nine decimal places, down
to the act chosen.

Run the same pair while the earlier events are still being felt and they do differ, but the
difference is in what the person is *carrying*, not in what this event stirred: the event's own
appraisal is identical in both, and only the left-over feeling from the earlier events moves.
That residue is gone within the hour. It is mood, not history.

## 5. What this predicts, and what it rules out

The architecture should show **two channels with opposite characters**: a graded one, where a
belief a motivation rule reads moves behaviour a little at every step, and a categorical one,
where a belief only interpretation reads moves nothing at all until a reading flips and then
moves everything at once.

It also rules out the shape of a patch. Any mechanism that carried history as a hidden modifier
into behaviour would move behaviour smoothly with the modifier. If the response curve is flat
and then steps, what is being measured cannot be that.

## 6. What the diagnosis does not settle

- Whether a reading can be made to flip at all by an amount of history a person could plausibly
  accumulate, and how much that is.
- Whether a changed reading, having changed the feelings and the wants, ever changes the act.
- Whether any of it survives a morning in which everybody else is also acting.
