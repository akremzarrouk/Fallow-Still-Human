## B. Diagnosis

Measured with a counterfactual harness: two mornings on the same seed, same world,
same people, differing only in the night. The night **did** reach motivation at
minute zero: the culprit's wish to be elsewhere rose by about 0.2, traceable to the
night. It was lost afterwards, in four places.

1. **A leak.** Every event faded every mind's feelings, including minds that never
   perceived it. People asleep in another room woke up with different feelings and
   different wants. S1's leak tests checked memories and grudges, never feelings, so
   they could not see it. It also means part of S1's 0.04 was this artifact.
2. **The belief stage was skipped.** Feelings fade and recall of a moment fades. Belief
   is the one stage that lasts, and the night moved no belief about what happened. So
   the only carrier was feeling, drained within about twenty minutes.
3. **Saturation.** Elena's night pushed her want to keep the house together, the push
   was in her trace, and it moved the number by nothing: both worlds at 0.995.
4. **Deliberation.** When a want did change, the decision often did not, because
   standing wants' proposals outscore it. Recorded, not targeted.

## F. Causal examples

From `causal-examples.md`, found by search. Arithmetic cut from the lines.

**Mara ate it. Seed 1, minute 71.** Without the night she sits with her mother. With it
she walks to her own room.

```
Motive [mara]: wants find_out 0.53   memory of threat about missing_can 0.80 x 0.35 = +0.28
  because Experience [mara]: kept it as threat, felt as fear
    because Interpretation [mara]: read it as threat (0.90), runner-up concern 0.60
      because Event: Elena counts the pantry with everyone standing there.
      because BeliefChange [mara]: answerable_for(mara, missing_can)
        because Experience [mara]: kept it as took_what_was_not_mine, felt as fear
          because Event: In the night Mara eats a can sitting on the kitchen floor.
```

**Elena gave it away. Seed 1, minute 71.** Without the night she waits. With it she
goes through the kitchen.

```
Motive [elena]: wants find_out 0.58
  because Experience [elena]: kept it as threat, felt as anxiety
    because Interpretation [elena]: read it as threat (0.90)
      because Event: Leo goes through the kitchen.
      because BeliefChange [elena]: answerable_for(elena, missing_can)
        because Experience [elena]: kept it as protect, felt as anxiety
          because Event: Mara is still awake and crying. Elena opens a can and gives it to her.
```

**Leo ate it (held-out). Seed 1, first minute.** Wish to be elsewhere 0.13 without the
night, 0.55 with it. First decision identical: he opens the pantry either way. His
shame leads straight back to the night; his fear leads back to the night and to several
other things, because fear is one number with every cause folded in.

## G. Negative findings

1. **Daniel barely changes.** Lasting effect 0.022, behaviour not above noise. The
   carrier works by re-reading other people's searches, and Daniel is the one searching.
2. **Hiding the can does almost nothing at the start** (0.004). An act done with a good
   conscience produces no feeling, so only the belief carries it, and only later.
3. **The carrier feeds a want with the wrong name.** In both decision-changing examples
   the want that leads is `find_out`, "needing to know what happened", held by someone
   who already knows. The chain is causally honest; the want it lands in is mislabelled.
   What it actually does is closer to watching for exposure.
4. **Knowing without being answerable still has no outlet.** Mara watching Elena give the
   food away changes nothing about Mara. Unchanged since S1.
5. **Feelings merge across causes.** Shame about being overruled and shame about stealing
   are one number. This caused the P1 miss, and it limits attribution by construction.
6. **The belief rule is specific to one topic and three acts.** Generalisation to other
   kinds of circumstance is unproven.
7. **Deliberation defects are untouched and still contaminate behaviour.** Genuine ties
   40.8%, pacing worse at 17, nobody eats.
8. **The saturation fix barely mattered** for the case that motivated it.
9. **The recall day filter** still hides last night from recall. It was not needed for
   this result and remains a latent defect.
10. **The suite is six times slower.**

## I. Recommendation

**MODIFY.**

The architecture carried the effect with four small changes and no redesign, so it is
not a REBUILD. Its core claim now has direct counterfactual evidence. But the evidence
also shows where it stops: a change in wants reaches behaviour only when it does not
have to compete with standing wants through a deliberation layer that is still 40%
coin flips and has no memory of intent. Leo wants to be elsewhere 0.42 more and opens
the pantry anyway. Daniel is ashamed and keeps searching.

Before S2, in this order:

1. **Intent persistence and the summed-appeal crowding in deliberation.** The main
   bottleneck between motivation and behaviour is now there.
2. **Keep feelings apart by cause**, or at least by concern, so a specific experience
   stays a specific experience.
3. **Rename or split `find_out`**, and let the belief carrier reach wants whose meaning
   matches it.
4. **Generalise the belief rule** from one topic to witnessed acts in general, as a test
   of whether the mechanism is a mechanism or a patch for this scenario.

## The question

**After S1.1, do we have evidence that the same simulated person can behave differently
because something happened to them, rather than because they have a different
personality?**

Yes, for some people, and the evidence is causal rather than correlational. Holding the
seed, the world and every person fixed, adding one night in which Mara eats the can
changes what she wants for the whole morning, on 20 of 20 seeds. Removing the night
restores her exactly. Removing only the belief that night formed removes most of the
lasting effect. Her behaviour then differs from the no-night control by more than the
noise between seeds, and that repeated on seeds never used to find it. Every want that
moved at the start walks back through belief, reading, feeling and memory to the night
itself. A held-out person nobody designed against moved the way the rules said he
would, in six of seven predictions. What remains unproven is almost everything that
would make this general. It is shown for one kind of circumstance and one belief rule,
and it does not reach Daniel at all. For Leo it changes wants without changing
decisions. It lands partly in a want whose name is wrong. And the deliberation layer
still decides, far too often, whether a changed person gets to act changed.
