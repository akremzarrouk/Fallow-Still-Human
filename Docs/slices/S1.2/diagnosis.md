# S1.2 diagnosis: what was actually wrong with deliberation

Written by hand from `audit-before.md` and `reach-before.md`, which were generated by
`S12DiagnosisTests` against the S1.1 code with only instrumentation added (structured
contributions on each scored option, a read-only decision hook, and a record of every
interruption). The instrument changed no behaviour: the full suite passed unchanged.

100 mornings, five design conditions, seeds 1 to 20, 7,960 decisions.

## 1. People are stopped far more often than anything warrants

**44.3% of all decisions are taken because the person was interrupted**, 35 interruptions
per morning. The rule reads the strongest feeling the person is *carrying*, not the
feeling the new event produced: anybody walking past stops anybody who is already upset.
**46.8% of interruptions came from events that themselves stirred less than the
threshold.**

The clearest case is in `reach-before.md`: Mara, seed 1, chooses to open the pantry at
minute 4, again at 7 and again at 10. Each time somebody else's action lands in the
last minute of hers, she drops it, and each time she chooses the same thing again.
Nothing changed her mind; she was simply never allowed to finish.

## 2. Nothing remembers why anybody walked anywhere

Of 639 completed walks, **46.8% were followed by something the want behind the walk did
not serve**. A walk is a means, and on arrival the decision is taken again from nothing.

## 3. Standing still is credited for what it avoids

Standing still came top in **45.2%** of decisions. All of its appeal came from two
proposals: `let_it_be` (wanting the house to hold, 62%) and `leave_it_alone` (wanting the
food to last, 38%).

Neither describes something standing still does. Both describe something it does not do:
it does not eat, and it does not go through anybody's things. But sitting with somebody,
opening the pantry, and walking to another room also do not eat, and most of them do not
confront anybody either. Only standing still is credited. That is a structural
advantage, not a preference anybody holds.

It shows in which wants decide anything. Weighing every decision again without one want:

| Want | Decisive in |
|---|---|
| `keep_peace` | 55.9% |
| `guard_supplies` | 49.7% |
| `find_out` | 29.1% |
| `look_after` | 27.2% |
| `restore_standing` | 14.0% |
| `get_food` | 12.3% |
| `avoid_exposure` | **1.9%** |

The two most decisive wants in the model are the two whose main outlet is standing
still. The want the night changes most is decisive almost never.

`stay_out_of_the_way` (not wanting to be looked at, when alone) has the same shape on
paper but is different in fact. Standing still is the only action that produces no event
anybody can perceive, so it is the only one that actually serves that want. It is left
alone.

## 4. Summing across wants is not itself the fault

**98%** of top options drew on two or more wants, and summing rather than taking each
option's strongest single reason changed which kind of action came top in **30.4%**. That
is what combining wants does; it is the intended behaviour, not a leak. The fault is in
what is being summed (section 3), not in summing.

Two suspected mechanical forms of double counting were measured and are absent: no top
option was credited twice by the same want (0.0%), and no option aimed at nobody was
credited once per person by the same kind of want (0.0%).

## 5. The seed settles too much

**48.3%** of decisions were settled by the seed, **41.2%** between genuinely different
kinds of action. This is a symptom of sections 1 to 3, not a separate mechanism, and it is
not targeted directly: the band is not touched.

## 6. What this does to a changed want

Mara's night raises her wish to be elsewhere, and that does reach her options: at
minute zero, both ways out of the kitchen score **+0.126** higher and eating scores
**−0.119** lower, averaged over 20 seeds. The best option never changes (0 of 20
seeds), because leaving is worth one want and the options above it are worth three or four.
Over the morning `avoid_exposure` was decisive in 16 of her 353 decisions with the night
and 22 of 343 without it. The night moves the candidates and does not move the choice.

## What is not wrong

- The ambiguity mechanism itself. It does what it says; it is being fed flat scores.
- `observe`. It came top in 4.7% of decisions, and its appeal came from wanting to know what
  happened (70%), wanting to be the one who settles it (17%) and wanting to see how somebody
  is (13%): things watching somebody actually does. No structural advantage found. Its one
  peculiarity is producing one near-identical option per person in the room, which shows up
  as ties between the same act aimed at different people, already reported apart from
  genuine ties.
- Candidate generation. Of 64,244 wants raised, 0.8% had no available option serving them.
