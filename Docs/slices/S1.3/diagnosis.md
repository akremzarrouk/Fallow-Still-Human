# S1.3 diagnosis: why nobody eats, and what eating would do if they did

Written by hand from `diagnosis-before.md`, generated by `S13DiagnosisTests` against the
S1.2 code with no change to the simulation. Shipped rules throughout, except where a
row says DIAGNOSTIC (a rule set that is never shipped) or names a changed circumstance.

## 1. How hunger is raised

Hunger is world state: a start level per person, plus 0.004 a minute times that
person's metabolism, clamped to 1. By minute 90 Daniel is at 0.96, Elena 0.91, Mara
0.84, Leo 0.81. Nothing but eating lowers it.

## 2. How hunger is represented as a want

One rule, `a_body_that_has_not_eaten`: `get_food` = hunger x 1.0, through the urgency
knee. So the want is the body, read fresh at every decision. **No want is stored
anywhere.** Every want is raised again from the person's state each time they decide.
So a want cannot stay active by itself; it stays active only while what it reads stays
the same.

The hunger term is the one kind of term that rests on no record. A feeling names its
causes, a memory names itself, a belief names its evidence. Hunger names nothing, so no
trace can say why somebody is as hungry as they are.

## 3. How eating affects hunger

When somebody eats, the world takes a portion and lowers their hunger by 0.45
(`SetHunger`). Seen where eating does happen, `get_food` fell by exactly that: Mara
0.606 to 0.160 under the diagnostic rules, and Daniel 0.821 to 0.376 under the shipped
rules with plenty of food.

**So the motivation does not remain incorrectly active after eating.** It falls at
once, by what the body got, with no timer and no decay. The lifecycle for food already
exists, physically. It has simply never run, because nobody has ever eaten.

## 4. Whether eating makes an experience, and whether that reaches the want

- Eating makes an `eat_portion` event. The eater perceives it and keeps a memory of it.
- Morning acts carry no intention, so the eater reads their own eating through the rules
  for watching somebody else. Where supplies are short, Mara read her own eating as
  "challenge, felt as anxiety", and it raised her belief that supplies are short.
- **Nothing links the fall in `get_food` to the eating.** Walking the trace from her want
  afterwards never reaches the eating. What does reach it is guarding the supplies and
  keeping the peace, through that belief and that anxiety. The consequence reaches the
  wants eating works against, and not the want it served.

## 5. Failure leaves nothing behind

- **Going for food and not eating.** In 100 mornings there were 262 walks to the food,
  216 of them Daniel's. Every one ended in "not worth what it costs here". At the next
  decision `get_food` was 0.019 higher, which is just the body getting hungrier. Nothing
  records that the person went and did not eat.
- **Reaching for food that is gone.** Emptying the pantry while somebody is eating
  produces no event and no memory. It is written on the action record ("found nothing
  left to eat") and nowhere else, so nobody perceives it, including the person who reached.

## 6. Why eating loses: the price, not the want

What eating costs each person alone, against the most `get_food` can be worth to them
this morning:

| | Price alone | Price watched | Best `get_food` this morning | Best score alone | Standing still alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daniel | 0.775 | 1.117 | 0.930 | +0.155 | 0.724 |
| Mara | 0.816 | 1.366 | 0.842 | +0.026 | 0.709 |
| Elena | 1.290 | 1.715 | 0.897 | −0.393 | 0.786 |
| Leo | 1.423 | 1.948 | 0.810 | −0.613 | 0.790 |

Three things together:

1. **For Elena and Leo, eating costs more than the top of the hunger scale is worth**
   (0.945 after the knee), even alone. Their values, honesty and belief that supplies
   are short price it above anything hunger can reach. No circumstance the house can
   produce makes it worth it to them.
2. **For Daniel and Mara it can be worth something, but barely,** and only alone. It is
   never worth more than standing still, whose value is mostly the credit S1.2 proved
   unearned. Watched, it is never worth anything to anybody.
3. **Most of the price comes from believing the shelf is short.** The opening count
   teaches everybody that. With plenty of food but the count still short, nobody eats.
   With plenty and a count that is not short, Daniel eats in 41 of 50 mornings, and Mara
   eats when she is starving and alone (16 of 20).

In the shipped scenario eating was an option in 7,738 decisions. Eating scored above
zero in none of them, beat standing still in none, and came top in none.

## What this means for S1.3

- The hypothesis, that a want stays equally active after the person acts on it, is
  **false for food as the code stands**: eating lowers it at once.
- The real gaps in the lifecycle are:
  - the fall is untraceable;
  - a failed reach is invisible;
  - going and not eating leaves no record.
- Why nobody eats is price. That is deliberation and content, which S1.3 is not allowed
  to change, so the lifecycle cannot be tested inside the scenario as written. It has to
  be tested in circumstances where eating happens, and the scenario as written has to be
  reported as still not letting anyone eat.
