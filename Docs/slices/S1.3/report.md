## 1. What was actually demonstrated

- **Eating updates the want to eat, and the update can be walked back to the eating.** For
  all 41 meals, every link of need, want, decision, eating, consequence, experience,
  outcome and the next want is a real parent link in the trace.
- **The update comes from the consequence, not from time.** Remove only what a portion does
  to the body, on the same seed. Nothing differs until the first meal. At that minute
  the eater, and only the eater, diverges. The partly satisfied outcome becomes an
  unresolved one, and the fall in the want is gone.
- **Different outcomes behave differently, for reasons in what happened:**
  - Eating left the want at what the meal left plus what the body added since, and the
    want names the meal.
  - Reaching for food that was gone left the want where it was (0.882 against 0.436 in
    the control). It was perceived, by the person and by the room.
  - Walking to the food and not eating left the want unchanged, and recorded why.
  - A want that vanished on the way was recorded as no longer relevant.
- **Having eaten makes eating again worth less:** 41 of 41 eaters, by a mean of 0.432 (one
  portion is 0.45). There were no second meals in 50 mornings with plenty of food.
- **The record of an outcome moves no want.** The same body raises the same urgency with or
  without it, and a test checks that to exact equality. There is no decay, no bonus and no timer.

What was **not** demonstrated:
- **Nobody eats in the scenario as written.** That was the failure that motivated this
  slice, and it is unchanged: the cause is price, and price was out of scope.
- **Where eating does happen, it happens almost only through S1.2's intention.** Across
  every circumstance measured, 171 of 187 meals were chosen on arriving at the kitchen
  under an intention to get food. The other 16 were all Mara, placed alone in the
  kitchen at hunger 0.99. That was the only case where eating came top when
  everything was weighed openly.

## 2. What was assumed

- **Hunger is the only need, and the body carries satisfaction.** There is no separate
  satiation state; a meal changes the body, and the want reads the body.
- **Satisfied means the need is at the bottom of its scale; partly satisfied means it is
  lower and above the bottom.** Nothing about a person enters that judgement.
- **An act is judged only against the wants it was chosen for, and only wants that read
  the need it touched.** Walking and checking the pantry are not judged, except when
  an intention carried across a walk is given up on arrival.
- **The eater's experience of eating is their existing reading of the `eat_portion` event.**
  Morning acts carry no intention, so the eater reads their own eating through the rules
  for watching somebody else. That gap predates S1.3 and was left alone.
- **Reaching for food that is gone is read only by the existing rules** (bad news, so
  concern). No interpretation or belief rule was added for it.
- **Experimental circumstances, not rules:** plenty of food and a count that is not short;
  one or two portions; somebody placed starving and alone in the kitchen; the body made
  less hungry, or the pantry emptied, part way through eating; and, for one test only,
  nobody's metabolism burning.

## 3. Diagnosis before the change

Full version in `diagnosis.md`. In short:

1. **Hunger is world state**, rising by 0.004 a minute times each person's metabolism.
2. **The want is the body, read fresh at every decision.** `get_food` = hunger, through the
   knee. No want is stored anywhere, so a want cannot stay active by itself.
3. **Eating already lowered the want at once**, by exactly the 0.45 a portion takes off
   hunger. So the motivation did not remain incorrectly active after eating. Nobody had
   ever eaten, so it had never been seen.
4. **Nothing linked that fall to the eating.** Hunger was the only kind of term resting on
   no record. What the eating did reach was the wants it works against (guarding the
   supplies, keeping the peace), through the belief that the shelf is short.
5. **Failure left nothing behind.**
   - 262 walks to the food in 100 mornings, 216 of them Daniel's, all given up on arrival,
     with no record anywhere.
   - Reaching for food that was gone was not an event, so nobody perceived it.
6. **Why eating loses is price.** Alone, eating costs Daniel 0.78, Mara 0.82, Elena 1.29
   and Leo 1.42. The most `get_food` can be worth this morning is 0.81 to 0.93. For Elena
   and Leo, eating costs more than the top of the hunger scale. Standing still, worth
   about 0.7, beat eating every time for everybody. In 7,738 decisions where eating was
   possible, it came top 0 times.

## 5. Experiment results

Shipped rules unless stated. "Plenty" is ten portions and an opening count that is not
short.

**A. Successful satisfaction** (plenty, five conditions, seeds 1 to 10)

- 41 meals, all partly satisfied, each taking 0.45 off hunger (for example 0.89 to 0.44,
  and 0.75 to 0.30).
- At the eater's next decision, `get_food` was exactly the knee of the body, and lower
  than when they chose to eat, every time.
- The want rested on the meal, and walked back to the eating event, every time.
- Satisfied: with the body made less hungry during the meal (0.30), the portion covered
  all of it. The outcome was satisfied, and `get_food` was not raised at the next decision.
- Eating again: every eater who was near food again later found eating worth less, by a
  mean of 0.432. There were no second meals.

**B. Partial satisfaction**

- Every meal is partial, because one portion is less than the hunger of anybody hungry
  enough to take one.
- On all 41, the want ten or more minutes later was exactly what the meal left plus the
  body since, and it named the meal.
- **With a single portion, nobody eats at all.** The first count of one is a short count,
  so everybody comes to believe supplies are short (0.96) and eating is priced out. That
  is price, not the lifecycle.

**C. Blocked, unresolved, no longer relevant**

- **Blocked:** the pantry was emptied in Daniel's last minute of eating. Outcome: blocked,
  hunger 0.89 unchanged.
  - Daniel read reaching for nothing as concern, felt as anxiety, and Elena, Leo and Mara
    perceived it.
  - `get_food` at his next decision was 0.882, against 0.436 in the control that ate. It
    walked back to finding nothing.
- **Unresolved** (the scenario as written): 132 walks to the food given up on arrival,
  132 outcomes, all unresolved. The want was unchanged by them, and now says why.
- **No longer relevant:** the walker's hunger was set to zero on the way. The intention
  lapsed as no longer wanted, and the outcome was no longer relevant.

**D. Causality** (plenty; `mara_ate_it` and `elena_fed_mara`; seeds 1 to 5; with and
without what a portion does to the body)

- Nothing differs before the first meal, on all 10 pairs.
- The first difference is always the eater, at the minute the meal is resolved: hunger
  and wants.
- Outcome with relief: partly satisfied. Without: unresolved.
- Anybody else first differs 16 to 20 minutes later, or never.

## 9. Remaining failures

1. **Nobody eats in the scenario as written.** Unchanged and not addressed: the cause is price.
2. **Elena and Leo cannot eat under any circumstance.** Alone, with plenty and nothing
   short, eating still costs them 1.015 and 1.148. At the very top of the hunger scale
   that scores −0.070 and −0.203. Their values will let them starve.
3. **Eating happens almost only through S1.2's intention.** 171 of 187 meals were chosen on
   arrival, weighing only what serves the walk's want, at margins as thin as +0.016.
   Weighed openly, eating came top only for Mara starving and alone. S1.2 judged that
   mechanism to have almost no effect; for food, it is nearly the only way in.
4. **A single portion is never eaten**, because counting one teaches everybody that the
   shelf is short.
5. **Walking to the food and not eating repeats** (132 times in 50 mornings). By design, the
   want is unchanged when nothing about the need changed. The repetition is also how
   Daniel eventually eats. Whether a walk should keep being valued by a want the person
   has already declined to act on at that price is a means-and-end question S1.3 did not
   touch.
6. **The eater reads their own eating as if watching somebody else.** Where supplies are
   short that is "challenge, felt as anxiety". Morning acts carry no intention.
7. **Finding nothing teaches nothing.** It stirs concern, and no belief follows, so a
   person can walk back later for food they have already found gone.
8. **Only needs have a lifecycle.** The wants S1.2 found nothing ever satisfies (looking
   after somebody, keeping the peace) are untouched.
9. **Still failing from S1.2:** `S11ExperimentTests.WhetherTheBehaviourEffectRepeatsOnSeedsNeverUsedBefore`.

## 11. Recommendation

**KEEP.**

The lifecycle mechanism is small and changes no behaviour where nothing happens. It is
traceable end to end, and it is judged only by what happened. It is shaped to be reused:
any want that reads a need, and any act whose consequence the world resolves.

But nothing in this slice makes anybody eat in the house as written. The diagnosis says
why, and it is not the lifecycle. Keep it with that stated plainly.

## 12. What to test next

1. **Whether a need can ever outweigh standing values.** A need cannot be worth more than 1,
   while the price of an act can be worth more than 1. So for two of the four people,
   no amount of hunger is ever enough. That is a design decision about people, not a
   number to tune: should Leo starve rather than take food? Settle it first. It is the
   real blocker behind "nobody eats", and until it is decided no measure of eating means much.
2. **The same lifecycle on a want that reads no need,** starting with looking after somebody:
   does sitting with them change anything the want reads? S1.2 found wants that nothing
   satisfies; this would test whether an outcome can reach one through a memory or a belief.
3. **Means and end.** Should walking to the food keep being valued by a want the person has
   declined, at that price, twice already? Measure it before deciding, because the repeated
   walks are currently the route by which nearly every meal happens.
4. **Whether the eater should know their own intention when they eat,** instead of reading
   their own eating as a witness would.
