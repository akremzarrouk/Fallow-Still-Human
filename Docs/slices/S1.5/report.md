# Slice S1.5 report: traits and values as dispositions, not standing wants

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: complete, stopped for review. A narrow diagnostic experiment:
the shipped rules are unchanged, and the alternative is a switch they do not set.
Reproduce with `./run-tests.sh`. **317 tests, 315 pass, 2 fail**: the two regressions S1.4
left failing, unchanged (section 6).

**In short.** Traits and values as always-on wants (the shipped model, A) are the wrong model
of dispositions. They carry 53 % of all urgency, 37 % of wants have nothing that happened
behind them, and every choice to stand still is led by such a want. They also bury the effect
of what happens to a person. Letting traits and values scale a response instead of raising
one (B) makes the model far more causally coherent. It does not make behaviour better.
With the standing wants gone, people walk back and forth between ends they have already
found not worth pursuing, and they become less different from each other. The standing wants
were not only wrong; they were holding the architecture's missing pieces out of sight.

---

## 1. What was actually demonstrated

- **On the shipped rules, traits and values behave as standing goals, and they decide a large
  share of behaviour.**
  - 53.0 % of urgency comes from them.
  - 34.6 % of wants are raised on traits and values alone.
  - Standing still is chosen in 52.9 % of decisions, and not once is it led by a want carried
    by what happened.
  - Taking the trait and value part out of wants changes the top option in 42.2 % of
    decisions.
  - With nothing having happened at all, each person still wants something and does
    something characteristic. Daniel searches a room where nothing is missing.
- **The alternative can be built without new numbers or new structure.** It is one switch.
  Traits and values become `C × (1 + D)`: a response to what the rule responds to, never a want
  on their own. Bench tests show exactly that arithmetic: the same circumstance is scaled by
  the disposition, and no circumstance raises nothing.
- **Under B, wants rest on what happened.**
  - 90.8 % of wants are carried mostly by circumstance (A: 33.4 %).
  - None is raised on traits and values alone.
  - 96.2 % rest on a record leading back to an event or the body (A: 62.6 %).
  - These held on the held-out case (P1, P2).
- **Under B, removing a circumstance removes the behaviour it drove.** With nobody's distress
  visible, sittings fall to 8 of 526 under B, and to 238 of 601 under A. The standing part of
  looking after somebody kept 40 % of A's sittings going with nothing to respond to.
- **Under B, what happens to a person stands out from the noise far more.** The night against
  no night, over the noise floor: Daniel 0.7 → 1.8, Mara 3.4 → 9.5, Daniel (hid it) 0.5 → 1.2,
  Elena 1.7 → 9.3. On the held-out case, Leo 3.6 → 5.7 (P9).
- **Under B, behaviour is worse in a specific, traceable way.**
  - Walks go from 410 to 1,048, and 72 % of them end in something the walk was not for.
  - Pacing rises from a mean of 0.43 to 2.74, with a worst case of 15.
  - The cause is traced (section 4): two wants whose ends are priced out at the destination,
    each proposing a walk from wherever the other left the person.
- **Under B, people are less different from each other.** The mean gap is 0.526 → 0.405, and
  the weakest condition 0.516 → 0.338. The closest pair moves apart (0.104 → 0.158), because
  Elena and Leo stop being two people standing still.

What was **not** demonstrated:
- That B is a better psychological model overall (section 8).
- That authored beliefs and never-fading memories should also be dispositions. B leaves them
  as they are, and they are what remains standing.
- That any particular form of B is the right one. `C × (1 + D)` was declared in advance as the
  smallest principled form, and no other was tried as a candidate.

## 2. What was assumed

- **What counts as a disposition:** trait, value and perceptiveness terms in motivation rules.
  Everything else stays in C: needs, memories, feelings, beliefs (evidenced or authored),
  ledger entries.
- **The form `C × (1 + D)`.** With no disposition, the response is the circumstance alone; with
  no circumstance, nothing. The factors are the shipped ones. D is not normalised, so a
  strongly disposed person can respond to a circumstance at nearly twice its size.
- **Circumstance vs history vs authored**, for the instrument, is decided by what a term read
  and whether it has records behind it. A belief's confidence mixes an authored prior with
  evidence and cannot be split. An evidenced belief counts wholly as history.
- **Costs, interpretation and appraisal keep their traits and values.** They were already
  dispositions in the sense tested, and the brief put costs out of scope.
- **The instruments.** The S1.2 audit (seeds 1–10), the S1 batch (seeds 1–40), and the S1.1
  noise measure (20-seed pools). "Decisions changed by taking X out" is a static re-weighing at
  the moment of the decision, not a re-run morning.
- **Held-out:** `leo_ate_it`, seeds 101–140, never run by any test before.

## 4. What changed behaviour, and why

**Standing still.** Under A, standing still was carried by two standing wants, `let_it_be`
(keeping the peace) and `leave_it_alone` (guarding the supplies). Both are mostly traits and
values. Under B, keeping the peace exists only while somebody is anxious, and guarding the
supplies scales from a belief. Standing still halves, and every remaining choice to stand
still is led by a want carried by circumstance.

**Walking and pacing.** `walks.md` takes every walk apart. Under B, of 1,218 walks:
- **305:** walked to look for something (`find_out`), found that searching there was not worth
  its cost on arrival, and walked off for food.
- **243:** walked to the food, found eating was not worth its price on arrival, and walked off
  to look for something.

Leo on `daniel_ate_it` seed 9 does this from minute 25 to minute 89:

| Minute | Room | Chose | For | Intention brought in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | kitchen | walk to the back room | find_out | |
| 29 | back room | walk to the kitchen | get_food | find_out: not worth what it costs here |
| 33 | kitchen | walk to the back room | find_out | get_food: not worth what it costs here |
| ... | ... | ... (the same, every four minutes) | ... | ... |
| 89 | kitchen | walk to the back room | find_out | get_food: not worth what it costs here |

Two things make this possible, and neither is new in S1.5:
1. **A walk is worth what its want is worth, however many times its end has been found
   unaffordable at the destination.** S1.3 records a lapse for hunger and lets it move
   nothing, by design. For `find_out` nothing is recorded at all. S1.3 already listed this as
   a means-and-end question it did not touch.
2. **The memory of the opening count never fades.** The opening is a scripted event with minute
   −1, and recall treats a memory with no minute as fully fresh. So `find_out` presses at the
   same strength all morning, for everyone, in A and B (Leo's 0.46 at every decision above). It
   is a circumstance that never ends, which is a standing want in all but name.

Under A, Leo's standing wish to keep the peace made standing still worth about 0.55. That beat
both walks, so the loop never showed. The ablation says where it comes from: B0 removes only
the wants with nothing behind them and paces almost as much (mean 2.29, worst 13). The pacing
follows from removing standing wants at all, not from the scaling.

**People becoming less different.** Under A, much of what told Elena and Leo from Daniel and
Mara was what they wanted with nothing happening. Under B:
- **Elena:** stays still while anxious, which her disposition makes her often.
- **Leo:** was defined by standing still. He is now defined by the loop above, and so is Mara,
  which is why they are the closest pair.
- **Daniel:** hardly changes, because his wants rest on memory and an authored belief.

**Eating.** Under A, eating had to beat a standing still that was worth something for everybody.
Under B it sometimes does, for Daniel, in the scenario as written (20 meals in 200 mornings).
S1.3 put "nobody eats" down to price. That was only half the account: price mattered because of
what standing still was worth against it.

**The night standing out.** A's standing urgency is the same with or without the night. It
added the same amount to every want in both worlds and diluted the difference. B's urgency
is all response, so what the night did is a larger share of everything the person wants.

## 5. What failed

1. **B's behaviour.** Walks that end in something else rise from 45.9 % to 72.4 %, mean pacing
   from 0.43 to 2.74, and worst pacing from 7 to 15. A calm, rational person (Leo) spends the
   morning walking between two rooms. That is not a more plausible person.
2. **Personality differentiation under B.** The mean gap is 0.405 (A 0.526), the weakest condition
   0.338, and held-out mean −0.057 (P7 missed by its own margin). It does not collapse (the
   closest pair moves apart), but a quarter of the difference between people was standing
   goals, and B has nothing to put in its place. Part of what differs now is a loop, not a
   person.
3. **Removing the missing can does not remove searching under B.** Rooms searched fall 35 %,
   not to zero. An authored belief (Daniel, Elena) and the never-fading opening memory keep
   `find_out` raised. The hypothesis did not cover them, and they are what remains standing.
4. **Five of nine held-out predictions missed** (section 7).
5. **The S1.4 regressions are not addressed**, as the brief required: the pacing gate still
   fails on A.

## 6. Regressions

- **The shipped behaviour is unchanged.** The rules do not set the switch. A's batch is identical
  to S1.4's attribution run: 16,060 decisions, gap 0.526, closest pair 0.104, pacing 7, ties
  35.4 %. **The full suite regenerates every file it writes for S1, S1.1, S1.2, S1.3 and S1.4
  byte-identical to the S1.4 commit, and fails on exactly the two tests S1.4 left failing**
  (the pacing gate and the emergent-moment fading check).
- **Code changed outside the experiment, with no effect on any run:**
  - `ScalerTerm` records its kind.
  - The S1.2 audit takes an optional second observer.
  - `Motivator` routes the sum through `Weigh`, which in the default mode is the same
    expression as before.
- `run-tests.sh` timeout 2,400 s → 3,600 s: the suite grew by about 15 minutes of S1.5 batches.

  **P8** missed on one of two halves each.

Each miss is pinned in `S15HeldOutTests` as recorded, to be turned round if it changes.

## 8. A better psychological model, or merely better metrics?

Neither, cleanly.

- **The causal model of dispositions is better, and the evidence is not just a metric chosen
  to flatter it.**
  - A said a calm person wants the peace kept when nothing threatens it, and a controlling
    person wants to search when nothing is missing. B says temperament is how strongly you
    respond.
  - The consequences B predicts were tested and held: removing a circumstance removes its
    behaviour, and a night that happened stands out, on design and held-out seeds alike.
  - Those are properties a psychological model should have, and A does not have them.
- **The behaviour is worse, and that is not a metric artefact either.** Leo pacing is visibly
  wrong. The better causal model produces a less believable person because the rest of the
  architecture relied on standing goals:
  - to keep people still when nothing called on them;
  - to hide wants whose ends a person has already found out of reach;
  - to hide a memory that never fades.
- So **B is not "better metrics"**: the behavioural metrics got worse. It is a better model of
  one stage, sitting on stages that were quietly depending on the old one.

**Is the current representation of motivation fundamentally wrong? Yes, in one respect this
experiment demonstrates, and incomplete in two it exposed.**
- **Wrong:** traits and values as permanent additive goals. They produce most of standing still,
  a large share of what distinguishes the cast, and wants with no cause. They also dilute every
  circumstance effect. This should not be kept as the model.
- **Incomplete:**
  1. A want re-raised from state at every moment has no way to register that its end, here, was
     already found not worth it, so it keeps proposing the same walk.
  2. Nothing represents a person with nothing calling on them. Under A the standing goals played
     that part.

Neither can be patched by tuning B. Giving standing still a baseline appeal, or raising the
price of walking, would be the kind of fix the brief rules out, and would hide the same
problems again.

## 10. Recommendation

**MODIFY.**

| Component | Decision |
|---|---|
| The source instrument (`MotivationSources`, the audit hook) | **KEEP** |
| Traits and values as standing wants (A) | **ABANDON as the model.** It stays the shipped default only because nothing that replaces it works yet. |
| Traits and values as dispositions (B, `respond`) | **MODIFY.** Keep it as the direction and the switch; do not ship it until the pacing it exposes has a cause-based answer. |
| The gate (B0) | **ABANDON**. The bench shows it lets the whole standing part back in on any trace of a circumstance. |

Not REBUILD: the switch is a few lines, and B fitted the existing pipeline without a new
abstraction. Not KEEP: shipping B now would trade a wrong model for visibly wrong behaviour.

**Recommended next experiment, after review:**
1. **Means and end under B.** When a person arrives and finds the end they walked for not worth it
   here, what should that change? Diagnose first, on B, where the loop is plain. Today a lapse
   changes nothing, for food by design and for looking into things because nothing is
   recorded. The question is what a person learns from having gone and declined, not how to
   make walking cheaper or standing still dearer.
2. **Before it, or as its first step: the opening memory that never fades.** It is a small,
   separate defect (minute −1 read as fully fresh), and it keeps `find_out` standing in both
   models. It should be diagnosed on its own so that it is not mistaken for part of the
   means-and-end result.

Stopped here for review. No further S1 feature was started.
