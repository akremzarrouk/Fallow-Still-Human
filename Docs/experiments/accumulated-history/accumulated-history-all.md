## report.md

### Accumulated interpersonal history: can it change what a later event means?

Date: 2026-09-20. Status: complete, stopped for review. **A small vertical experiment, not
S1.8 and not an audit.** Nothing in `Fallow.Core` and nothing in the data was changed: no rule,
number, mapping, action, emotion or threshold. The experiment is built on S1.7's harness, which
is ordinary scenario data.
Reproduce with `./run-tests.sh`. **394 tests, 392 pass, 2 fail**: the two regressions S1.4 left failing, unchanged (the pacing gate, still 7 on Model A, and the emergent-moment fading check). **Every generated document of S0 to S1.7 and the audit regenerated with identical content**; the only content change anywhere was the wall-clock seconds in the decision-sensitivity results, and one file differed in line endings only.

**In short. Yes, it changes the meaning. It still does not change the act.**

Daniel goes through Elena's things. With no history she reads it as **threat**, because she
believes she is answerable for the missing can and somebody is looking into it. After he has
done the same thing twelve times before, she reads the identical event as **disrespect**. What
it does to her changes completely with it: anxiety and fear become hurt, frustration, anger and
shame; her wish to find out collapses from 0.635 to 0.234 and a wish to get her standing back
appears. In a live morning this happens in **10 of 10 seeds**, with her whole morning up to that
moment identical.

And she does exactly the same thing afterwards, in **10 of 10**. Only the reason changes: she
checks the pantry for `find_out` without the history and for `get_food` with it.

The second subject shows the other half. Mara reads the same event as disrespect whatever her
history, because **her character file already says she believes Daniel treats her like a
child** (0.55). Twelve more experiences raise the weight of her reading from 1.260 to 1.401 and
change **nothing else whatsoever**: same emotions, same wants, same act, to the last decimal.

So the answer to the question the episode experiment left open is: the durable history can reach
meaning, but only by crossing a threshold, and past the threshold the difference dies at the
same place everything else does, in the many-to-one map from wants to acts.

---

#### 1. Hypothesis

**H1.** Repeated experiences with the same person can, through the existing memory, belief and
interpretation pipeline, give the same later event a different meaning than it has with no
history.

Primary endpoint: the **interpretation label**. Secondary: whether that difference survives into
appraisal, emotion, motivation and action.

#### 2. Exact scenario

Built from S1.7's harness (`S17`), which constructs ordinary events and applies them through
the ordinary pipeline:

1. **The history.** N times, each on its own day, Daniel goes through the subject's things while
   she stands there (`search_belongings`, topic `missing_can`). She perceives each one. N = 0, 1,
   2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16.
2. **What makes her answerable.** In the answerable arms she has given a can away in the night
   in front of somebody, which through the existing nudge leaves her believing she is answerable
   for what is missing. This is the same in every history level.
3. **Time.** 40 units of fading, which S1.7 established is enough that nothing the earlier
   events stirred is still felt. Only durable state survives into the later event.
4. **The later event.** Built identically in every arm: he goes through her things again, or he
   watches her. Same room, same company, same hunger, same access.

Two subjects: **Elena**, whose character file seeds neither of the beliefs this history moves,
and **Mara**, whose file seeds `tendency(daniel, treats_me_like_a_child)` at 0.55.

Then the same question inside a **live morning**: the history written into the backstory, the
same later event injected at the same minute, with her circumstances and her whole morning up to
that moment checked for equality.

#### 3. Conditions

| | |
|---|---|
| **No history** | N = 0 |
| **Small history** | N = 1 |
| **Accumulated history** | N = 12 (with the full curve to 16 measured) |

#### 4. Controls

| Control | Result |
|---|---|
| The same arm run twice | identical |
| History she was never there to see (the same events, no access) | identical to no history, in label and in everything downstream |
| History taken away again | identical to no history |
| Everything but N held constant | same room, same company, same hunger, same later event, same access, same fading |
| Randomness | the probe arms use one fixed stream; the live mornings are paired by seed, and the history lives in the backstory, so the morning's stream is untouched |
| The morning before the later event | checked decision by decision; runs whose morning had already diverged were **excluded**, not counted |

#### 5. Methodology note

The brief's CASE D — history changes an earlier act, and the later difference is cascade rather
than meaning — is a real risk in the live morning, because the history sits in the backstory and
can change her from the first minute. It was handled by requiring that her circumstances **and
every decision she had taken that morning** be identical across arms before a run counts. Ten of
Elena's ten runs passed that test. **None of Mara's did**, so none of her live-morning
differences are claimed here.

**The first divergence** is the label, at the moment of the later event. Everything before it is
identical by construction and by check.

#### 8. Failure analysis: where it stops, and why

The act does not change, and the reason is structural rather than accidental:

- The two readings produce **different wants** (`find_out` against `restore_standing` and
  `get_food`), and those wants **ask for the same acts**. `search_room` is credited by
  `find_out` at fit 0.80 and `restore_standing` at 0.60; `check_pantry` by four wants. The
  Action Representation Audit measured this; here it is the thing that swallows a completely
  changed inner life.
- For Mara the chain stops even earlier. Her reading does not change label, so only its
  **weight** moves, and weight is discarded at the argmax. Her downstream is identical to the
  last decimal across 16 lived experiences.

Neither stop is a bug in the history machinery. The history did its work: it reached the
meaning, changed it, and changed everything the meaning feeds.

#### 9. Interpretation, against the brief's cases

| Case | Which subject | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **A** history -> belief -> label -> appraisal -> wants -> **action** | nobody | the act never changed |
| **B** history -> belief -> **weight only** -> nothing downstream | **Mara**, all 16 levels | weight 1.260 -> 1.417, downstream identical |
| **C** belief not connected strongly enough to interpretation | nobody | the connection is strong; it is thresholded |
| **D** earlier divergence mistaken for later meaning | **Mara's live mornings**, excluded | her morning differed before the probe in 10 of 10 |
| **E** interpretation changes, behaviour does not because the difference is lost later | **Elena** | label, appraisal, emotion and wants all change; the act does not |

So the experiment lands on **E for Elena and B for Mara**, and identifies exactly which stage
eats the difference in each case.

#### 10. Limitations

- **Two subjects, one kind of history, one pair of later events.** The flip was found in one
  configuration: a subject with no seeded belief, made answerable, searched. Nothing here says
  how common that configuration is.
- **N=12 is where this history crosses this threshold.** It is not a general number: it is where
  a 0.4-weighted belief overtakes a 0.9 competitor.
- **The live-morning evidence is Elena's only.** Mara's was excluded by the cascade check, which
  is the honest outcome but leaves her live behaviour untested.
- **The "no history" condition is not a blank relationship for Mara**, because her file seeds the
  belief. Authored relationship and lived relationship are the same variable here, and the
  authored value dominates: her file supplies 0.22 of the reading instantly, while 16 lived
  experiences add 0.16.
- **The later event is the same kind of act as the history.** Whether history with somebody
  changes the reading of a *different* kind of act by them was not tested.

#### 11. Classification

| Claim | Label |
|---|---|
| Accumulated interpersonal history can change what a later identical event **means**, through the shipped mechanisms, with nothing added | **PROVEN** (Elena: threat -> disrespect at N=12, in the harness and in 10 of 10 live mornings) |
| The change of meaning carries into appraisal, emotion and motivation | **PROVEN** (completely different emotions and wants) |
| The change of meaning carries into action | **FAILED** (0 of 10 acts changed; only the reason did) |
| The effect is a threshold, not a gradient | **PROVEN** (eleven times change nothing; the twelfth changes the label; the belief underneath moves smoothly) |
| Where the label does not flip, history changes only a discarded weight | **PROVEN** (Mara: 16 levels, downstream identical to the last decimal) |
| It is her experience, not the world, that does it | **PROVEN** (history she never saw leaves her identical to no history) |
| The stage that eats the difference is the want-to-act map | **PLAUSIBLE**, strongly supported: the competing wants credit the same acts, and the act chosen is credited by both |
| The number 12 means anything in general | **UNPROVEN** |
| History with somebody changes how a *different* kind of act by them is read | **UNKNOWN**, untested |

#### 12. Recommendation

**KEEP.**

The mechanism under test works and needs no change: durable interpersonal history, built only
from what a character actually perceived, reached a later interpretation and changed it, and
everything that depends on meaning changed with it. That is the thing the episode experiment
could not show, and it is now shown.

Keep it as it is, and do not add a relationship score, a history strength, or a memory-to-action
rule. The next bottleneck is not in this machinery.

#### 13. Next step

Not implemented, and the smallest one the evidence justifies: **the act that a changed meaning
should have changed.**

Elena, after twelve intrusions, reads the thirteenth as a slight rather than a danger, feels
hurt and anger instead of fear, wants her standing back instead of wanting to know — and checks
the pantry either way, because every want she could have asks for the same acts. The question
that follows is not about history at all. It is: **what act does `restore_standing` have that
`find_out` does not?** Today, none.

That is one measurement on the existing proposal data, and it is the same finding the Action
Representation Audit reached from the other direction. If those two agree, the next thing worth
changing in Fallow is the map from wants to acts, not memory, not belief, and not
interpretation.

## predictions.md

### Accumulated interpersonal history: predictions

Written **before the experiment was run**, and kept as written.

The question: can repeated experiences with the same person change what a later event **means**,
using only the shipped mechanisms?

The design: N earlier times Daniel goes through the subject's things (N = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8,
10, 12, 16), each its own day, each perceived by her; then time enough that nothing is still
felt; then one later event, built identically in every arm. Two subjects (Elena, as in S1.7, and
Mara, as in the episode experiment), two later events (he goes through her things again; he
watches her), and the subject either answerable for the missing can or not.

| # | Prediction | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | At N = 0 the relevant beliefs about Daniel are 0 | they are not |
| P2 | At N = 1 the existing nudges produce a non-zero belief, with no rule written for this experiment | nothing moves |
| P3 | The belief accumulates with N, each step adding less than the last, and never reaching 1 | it is flat, linear, or reaches 1 |
| P4 | The later event is perceived identically in every arm (same access, same event) | access or the event differs |
| P5 | For at least one subject and one later event, the winning **label** differs between two history levels | the label is the same at every N, everywhere |
| P6 | Where the label does not change, the **weight** rises monotonically with N | the weight is flat |
| P7 | Where the label changes, appraisal, emotions and wants change with it, with no mechanism added | the label changes and nothing downstream does |
| P8 | Where the label changes, the **act** chosen at that moment usually does not (S1.7 found 2 of 27, both ties) | the act changes in most circumstances |
| P9 | In a live morning, any later behavioural difference is mostly cascade or tie-breaking and cannot be attributed to the history without tracing | every difference traces cleanly to the belief |
| P10 | Removing the history restores the N = 0 result exactly | it does not |

**Subject-specific, committed in advance:**

- **Elena**: the flip exists. Her reading of being searched should go from `threat` (she believes
  she is answerable for the missing can) to `disrespect`, somewhere near N = 12, reproducing
  S1.7.
- **Mara**: I expect **no flip**. From the rules, her `disrespect` reading of being searched
  starts at about 1.04 (0.50 base, 0.375 from valuing autonomy, 0.165 from pride) while the
  competing `threat` cannot exceed her belief that she is answerable. If so, accumulated history
  only raises a reading that was already winning.
- **Being watched**: no flip for anybody, because only one rule reads that event, so there is
  nothing for history to overtake.

**What would make this a major result:** P5 holding *and* P7 holding *and* the act changing in a
live morning by a path that traces back to the belief. **What would reproduce the known
bottleneck:** P5 failing while P6 holds, which would mean history changes only a weight that the
deliberator discards.

#### Where the causal chain stops

For Elena the chain runs: earlier events -> perceived -> read as disrespect -> two belief nudges
-> belief accumulates -> later identical event -> **different label** -> different appraisal ->
different emotions -> different wants -> **the same act**.

It stops at the act, and the reason is the one the Action Representation Audit named: the wants
that the two readings produce (`find_out` against `restore_standing` and `get_food`) ask for the
same acts. In the live morning her act is `check_pantry` either way; all that changes is which
want is credited with it.

For Mara the chain stops one stage earlier, at the label: the weight of her reading rises from
1.260 to 1.417 and nothing downstream moves at all, because weight is discarded once the
argmax has chosen.
