and no rule, number or line of `Fallow.Core` was changed. The shipped data was switched to the
primary baseline (three settings), every earlier test now selects Model A explicitly, and the
experiment only re-weighs decisions the morning actually took.

failing, unchanged (the pacing gate, still 7 on Model A, and the emergent-moment fading check).
All 14 new tests pass. **Every generated document of S0 to S1.7 and the audit regenerated

**In short.** Motivation reaches deliberation almost everywhere. The trouble is that the act
barely responds to it:
- Nearly every want has something here it could make happen: only 1.9 % of want and decision
  pairs have no candidate act.
- A typical want has to move by about a third of its whole possible range before another act
  becomes preferred (median 0.36), and by nearly half before another act is chosen without the
  seed (median 0.46).
- A change of 0.10 makes another act certain in 2.5 % of the pairs that can change the act at
  all.

The motivational difference is not lost at candidate generation. It is lost in three places,
all in the step from wants to acts, and it did not stay lost in any single one:
1. **Many wants to few acts.** Seven wants credit seven kinds of act with heavy overlap, and
   wants that differ in the mind ask for the same acts. `restore_standing` is exactly three
   quarters of `find_out` on every act they share (*not exactly: see the erratum*).
2. **Hub acts that sum everything.** Checking the pantry is credited by four wants and standing
   still by three. Standing still also costs nothing, so it is what remains whenever nothing
   else is worth its price.
3. **An ambiguity band 0.08 wide in score.** Converted into urgency, that is a window a median
   0.18 wide in which the act belongs to the seed.

S1.7's history is the proof: it moved Elena's wants by a median 0.40 under B. Any one of those
changes alone would have changed her preferred act in 22 of 27 circumstances. All of them
together changed it in 5, because they moved urgency between wants that ask for the same acts.
Interpretation strength would add more of exactly that kind of difference upstream of the same
compression. The evidence points at the want-to-act mapping, not at the strength of what feeds
it.

## 1. Executive summary

**Part 1 established the baseline.** The shipped data now runs Model B with both S1.6 fixes,
and it is S1.6's `B timed end` condition decision for decision (25 mornings, 1,853 decisions,
identical to the last bit). Model A is kept as a selectable comparison. As selected, it is the
runtime that shipped before, decision for decision: 15 mornings fingerprinted on the
pre-change data. No `Fallow.Core` code was touched.

**Part 2 measured the transfer function from motivation to action.** It took every decision of
15 real mornings (seed 1 of the five design conditions, under three architectures, 1,215
decisions). It moved each want the rules could raise there across its whole possible range,
alone, with everything else held identical: mind, percept, other wants, intention, random
stream. That is 10,050 want and decision pairs, re-weighed 330,105 times by the unchanged
deliberator. Every decision re-weighed unchanged reproduced the morning's own to 1e-12. Under
the primary baseline:

| Of 3,001 want and decision pairs | Share |
|---|---|
| Moving the want within its possible range changes the preferred act | 56.9 % |
| The want already adds to the act, and no strength of it changes the act | 18.1 % |
| The want adds to an act that is never preferred at any strength it can have | 20.6 % |
| An intention carried in holds the decision elsewhere | 2.5 % |
| Nothing here serves the want (candidate generation) | 1.9 % |

| Where the act does change, the nearest change | Median (quartiles) |
|---|---|
| ... that makes another act *possible* (it enters the band) | 0.268 (0.125 to 0.349) |
| ... that makes another act *preferred* | 0.355 (0.206 to 0.438) |
| ... that makes another act *certain* (clear of the band) | 0.462 (0.314 to 0.533) |

For scale: urgency can never reach 1 (the motivator's curves approach it). Over one morning,
the span between the 10th and 90th percentile of a person's own want runs from almost nothing
(`guard_supplies`, set by one belief) to about 0.7 (`look_after`), and is mostly 0.2 to 0.5
(`results.md` §1.9).

**What this demonstrates:**
- **Hypothesis A fails.** Small, causally reasonable changes of motivation do not reliably
  change the act.
- **Hypothesis C fails** as the explanation. Candidates are almost always generated.
- **B is supported.** Motivation reaches deliberation and is outweighed: by the act's own
  price in 46 % of the outweighed pairs and by other acts in 54 %.
- **D is supported in one specific form.** The ambiguity band turns every change of preference
  into a window the seed decides. Under Model A, S1.7's history changed the preferred act in 8
  circumstances and the chosen act in 2, so the seed absorbed at least 6.
- **E is only partly supported.** Alternatives exist: at 81 % of decisions, moving one want or
  another can make four or more different acts preferred, the present one included. But the
  acts they reach are few kinds, and one act, standing still, is free.

The largest single loss is none of the five hypotheses as stated. It is **action
representation**: the many-to-one mapping from wants to acts. It turns different reasons into
the same act and makes opposite movements of two wants cancel.

## 6. Experimental method

**The instrument** (`SensitivityMeasures.cs`). A decision the morning actually took is captured
in the `Decided` callback, while the mind is in the state it decided in. It is then weighed
again by a fresh `Deliberator` on the same rules, with:
- the same mind, percept and intention carried in;
- the same random stream (`new Rng(seed).Fork(who@minute)`, which is how the morning forks it);
- the same list of wants, except that **one** want's urgency is set to a value.

Details of the perturbation:
- A want set to 0 is left out, as the motivator leaves it out.
- A want that was not raised can be given an urgency, meaning it is treated as if raised that
  strongly.
- The list is re-sorted as the motivator sorts it. Every other want is the same object
  (`X0`).
- Nothing else is touched: no memory, belief, feeling, trait, value, room, person, rule or
  number.

**Faithfulness.** Before any perturbation, each decision is re-weighed unchanged and must
reproduce the original choice, resolution, commitment and every option's score to 1e-12. It
did for all 1,215 census decisions, the 361 replication decisions, all 60 first decisions
under the three architectures, and all 54 S1.7 probe decisions (asserted).

**What was moved.** At each decision, every want the rules could raise there: the six wants
about the situation, and `look_after` once per person present, whether raised or not.

**Range.**
- Urgency 0 to 0.995. The motivator's knee and accumulation approach 1 and never reach it, so
  that is every value a want can have.
- Then probes at 1.5, 3 and 10, which no want can have, only to tell "needs more than the
  scale allows" from "never".
- Census grid 0.1 (0, 0.1, ..., 0.9, 0.995); close-ups grid 0.01.
- Every change located to 0.001 by halving.

**Measurements**, for each want at each decision:
- the options it credits, at every point;
- the *preferred* option (first considered; no randomness);
- the option *chosen* with the morning's stream;
- the resolution, the tied options, and the commitment.

From these come three thresholds in each direction: another act becomes **possible** (a clear
decision gains a tie), **preferred** (the crossing), and **certain** (preferred and clear of
the band). Each pair is then classified; the classes are defined in `results.md` §1.2.

**Conditions**, under otherwise equivalent circumstances:
- **B** (primary);
- **A + S1.6 fixes**, which differs from B only in `dispositions`, and is the architectural
  comparison;
- **A** as shipped before, the reference.

**Scenarios and seeds**, all existing: the house, the cast, the backstory and the rules.

| Set | What was run |
|---|---|
| Census | the five design conditions (`daniel_ate_it`, `daniel_hid_it`, `mara_ate_it`, `elena_fed_mara`, `miscount`), seed 1, all three architectures |
| Replication | the same, seed 401, which no earlier measurement had used, primary only |
| First decisions | each person's first decision in each design condition, seed 1, all three architectures, grid 0.01 |
| Close-ups | the four first decisions of `daniel_ate_it` seed 1, grid 0.01, with the leading want's sweep. At every 0.05 it gives the share of 200 other streams (`Fork(who@minute#k)`) that choose the act actually taken |
| S1.7 bridge | S1.7 E6's 27 circumstances (3 rooms x 3 companies x 3 hungers), Elena with and without twelve earlier slights, under A (as S1.7 ran it) and B |
| Traces | chosen by a stated rule (`results.md` §6) |
| Proposal overlap | read from `decisions.json` |

**What was not done.** No joint perturbation of several wants, except in the S1.7 bridge,
where the joint change is the one history actually produced. No perturbation of anything
upstream of motivation. No change to any mechanism to make a transition appear.

| Hypothesis | Verdict |
|---|---|

**`get_food`, Daniel, same decision** (a want that moves it, but only downward):
- `hunger 0.55 x 1.00`, resting on the body alone (the Access record).
- It credits eat (1.0) and check_pantry (0.4). Eat is priced out (S1.3's costs).
- Lowered to 0.189 it removes enough from check_pantry that search_room ties it.

**Where the perturbed value lives** (code, confirmed by grep):
- It is **created** in `Motivator.Raise`, as the knee and combination of rule terms.
- It is **read** only in `Deliberator.Rank` (`contribution = motive.Urgency * proposal.Fit`,
  `Sim/Deliberator.cs`), including inside `Foresee` for a walk under `means: end`.
- It **affects** the appeal of every option a proposal of that want endorses, hence the score
  order, the band's membership and the weights of `PickWithin`, and whether an intention held
  (`serving[0].Score > 0`).
- It **stops** at the choice. `SilentMorning` takes the chosen option's kind, target,
  destination and duration, the intention's *key*, and the trace id. The act, the world event
  it produces and what it does to anybody are identical whether it won by 0.001 or by 1.0.

## 10. Information lost at boundaries

**Calculated and then discarded:**
- **The interpretation's weight.** Only the winning meaning reaches appraisal and the
  experience. The weight goes to `EventOutcome.InterpretationWeight`, which is reported and
  never read (audit; `Sim/Simulation.cs:254`).
- **How much one act beat another.** `Decision.Margin` goes into `ActionRecord` and the
  trace, and nothing reads it.
- **The score differences inside the band.** Anything under 0.08 becomes the weight of a draw
  (`PickWithin`). It is a preference no longer.
- **The strength of the want behind an intention.** `Intention` keeps the want's key. On
  arrival the want is raised again from scratch.
- **The strength of the chosen act's reasons.** Act execution reads only the option. The
  outcome of an act reads which wants contributed (`Amount > 0`), not how much.

**Stored but never read by the simulation:**
- `ActionRecord.LeadingUrgency`, `Margin` and `Outcome` (reports and batch only).
- `Motive.Terms`: read only to list the want's trace parents.
- `ScoredOption.Serves` and `Prices` (strings for the trace).
- `InterruptionRecord.StandingIntensity`: recorded, never compared. Whether an act is
  interrupted depends on how hard the new event lands (0.45), not on how badly the act was
  wanted.
- From the audit, not re-checked here: 5 of 8 ledger entry kinds; the `Emotion` and `Action`
  trace kinds, which are never written.

**Read but not propagated:**
- A want's urgency is read by every proposal that endorses an available option. Where one
  want credits both the preferred act and its alternative, only the **difference of the two
  fits** reaches the choice between them. For `find_out`, check_pantry against search_room is
  0.2; for `restore_standing` it is 0.15.
- Where several wants credit the same act, a change in one is diluted by the sum.

**In the trace but absent from decision logic:**
- The deliberation record's `foresaw`, `options`, `margin`, `tied`, `serves` and `against`.
- The motive record's `because`.
- Every reason is traceable. None of the magnitudes after the argmax is used.

## 11. Model A against Model B

Observed differences, not a ranking:
- **What there is to choose between.** Under B, 2.9 options per decision are worth more than
  nothing; under A, 5.7 to 6.1. Standing wants inflate every act they credit, so under A more
  acts are near each other and more decisions are ties (29 to 33 % against 20.6 %).
- **What standing still means.** Under A, standing still is *for* something: a standing wish
  to keep the peace or guard the supplies. Removing the leading want changes it 84 to 88 % of
  the time. Under B it is the free default when nothing is worth its price, and removing the
  leading want changes it 20 % of the time.
- **Transition distances.** B's median distance to another preferred act is slightly larger
  (0.355 against 0.32). Fewer B transitions are tiny (6.9 % at most 0.05, against 16.6 % under
  A), consistent with A having more near-ties (33 % of its decisions are the seed's). Which
  wants make A's tiny transitions was not broken down.
- **Leading wants are more expendable under B.** Removing the leading want changes the act in
  45 % of B's decisions against 81 to 84 %. Under B the act is more often overdetermined, or is
  standing still by default.
- **A want not raised is absent under B, present under A.** 1,162 unraised pairs against 144
  to 187. Given urgency from nothing, a B want changes the act 64 % of the time at a median
  0.40.
- **The same mind, the same act, a different reason.** At identical first decisions the
  architectures differ in the act 4 times in 20, always inside a tie, and in the reason 9
  times.
- **A + fixes and A barely differ at the decision level.** M1 and M2 act over a morning (where
  walks and memory age), not on the weighing of one decision.

## 13. Overfitting risks

- **One scenario.** One house, one missing can, four people, seven wants, seven kinds of act.
  The hub acts (check_pantry, wait) are hubs because of these proposals. A different act
  vocabulary could change every number here.
- **Few seeds.** The census is seed 1 of five conditions and the replication is seed 401. The
  distributions replicated closely; the share of decisions the seed settled did not (20.6 % against
  31.3 %).
- **The yardstick for "small".** The bins (0.05, 0.10, 0.25) are chosen. The natural spreads
  and the S1.7 history effects are offered as context, not as a standard.
- **Single-want perturbations.** Real causes move several wants at once, and the bridge shows
  joint moves can cancel. The census cannot see that. Only the bridge can, and it is one person
  and one kind of history.
- **Grid assumptions.** The census assumes no change of preference comes and goes inside one
  step of 0.1. 5.2 % of moving pairs under B change more than once across the range, so some
  thresholds may be the second change rather than the first. The close-ups use 0.01.
- **The first decision is unrepresentative.** Everyone is in the kitchen with everyone else,
  so it has the most options.
- **"Within reach" depends on two curves.** The knee at 0.85 and the combination cap the
  scale below 1. If the knee moved, "outweighed within the scale" would move with it.
- **Model A + fixes is a constructed condition.** It is two shipped switches combined, used
  only to isolate dispositions.

## 14. What this means for S1.8

Not implemented. The evidence does **not** support making interpretation-strength propagation
the next investigation. S1.7's history already produced want changes of a median 0.22 (A) and
0.40 (B). That is as large as the median change a single want needs to make another act
preferred (0.17 and 0.30), though in the needed direction only about a fifth of the time (19
and 23 of 108). In 18 and 22 of 27 circumstances at least one of those changes, alone, would
have moved the preferred act. The act chosen still changed in 2 to 3 of 27. The loss happens
after motivation:
- the changes moved urgency between wants that ask for the same acts;
- the acts they compete for are sums of several wants;
- the remaining preference changes fell inside the band.

Strengthening the input would send more of the same kind of difference into the same
compression.

The evidence supports investigating, in this order:
1. **Action representation: the mapping from wants to acts.** Which wants have no act of
   their own; whether restoring standing and finding out should share their acts in fixed
   proportion; what makes an act a hub; and whether standing still should be free. This is
   where S1.7's difference cancelled. It is a question about proposals and the act
   vocabulary, measured with this instrument, before any new act is added.
2. **Deliberation and scoring: the band.** The ambiguity band is 0.08 in score, and dividing
   by fits of 0.35 to 0.9 makes it 0.09 to 0.23 of urgency. That is as wide as many real
   differences between people, and it decides 20 to 31 % of decisions by seed.
3. **What happens after the argmax.** Nothing downstream knows how much the act was wanted.
   That is not why the act fails to change, but it is why two people doing the same act for
   very different strengths of reason look identical.

Candidate generation and motivation sensitivity do not need investigating first; the census
rules both out as the main cause here.

## 15. Recommendation

**MODIFY.**

Keep:
- the baseline (B with both S1.6 fixes, now shipped);
- the instrument;
- the direction of the work: understanding the machine before changing it.

Change the next step. The bottleneck S1.7 pointed at is real, but it is not upstream: it is in
how wants become acts. Interpretation strength should wait until the step from wants to acts
can carry the difference it would add.
