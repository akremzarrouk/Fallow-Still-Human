## report.md

### Action representation audit: does the reason survive the act?

Date: 2026-09-19. Status: complete, stopped for review. **An audit, not S1.8.** Nothing in
`Fallow.Core` and nothing in the data was changed. One read-only diagnostic test was added,
because the only existing counts of which want leads to which act (`Docs/audit/census.md`)
were measured on Model A, and Model A is no longer what runs. It watches 50 primary-baseline
mornings, plus the same 50 under Model A where stated, and writes `diagnostic.md`.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `diagnostic.md` | Generated by `ActionRepresentationAuditTests` (diagnostic only): what each act puts into the world, the same act for different reasons, whether the reason can be recovered, one want and many acts, standing still, hubs, and five cases boundary by boundary |
| `action-representation-all.md` | Both merged, by `merge-slice-docs.py` |

The Decision Sensitivity results (`Docs/experiments/decision-sensitivity/`) are used where

#### 1. Executive conclusion

**Action representation is still the most credible bottleneck, but the audit splits it in
two, and only one half is what the Decision Sensitivity report emphasised.**

**The half it now proves: the reason never crosses the act boundary.** Of 918 world events
the morning's acts produced, none carries an intent. No actor ever "knows their own
intention" for a morning act: 918 of 918 are read the way a witness would read them. Every
act's event record in the trace rests on nothing, so no memory of any act can be walked back
to why it was done. This is not a limit of the seven-act vocabulary or the silent house. It
happens at four specific places in the code. The architecture already has a working channel
for exactly this: an event's `intent` becomes its actor's memory, and four appraisal rules
read it. Authored history uses that channel; the morning passes `null`.

**The half it weakens: sharing a physical act is not itself the problem.**
- Different wants sharing an act is often legitimate. Checking the pantry is a sensible means
  for hunger, for guarding the supplies and for looking into the missing can.
- Hub acts are chosen while other kinds of act are also worth doing (100 % of the time for
  checking the pantry and for comforting), so the compression is not a lack of alternatives.

Sharing becomes destructive **because** the end is dropped: once the act is chosen nothing
records which end it served, so identical means make identical history. And three of the
seven wants (`find_out`, `restore_standing`, `keep_peace`) have no act that changes anything
they read. That is partly the scenario (nothing to find, nobody speaks), not the
architecture.

#### 2. Actual action architecture

No interpretation condition can read an event's `intent`: the vocabulary of conditions has no
such term. So a witness never sees a reason. What differs between witnesses is **the
witness**: their beliefs, relations, role, and whether they were the one addressed.

##### 2.4 A channel that exists and is not used

- Authored events carry `intent`, a `self_meaning`. There are six: `take_responsibility`,
  `share_information`, `protect`, `assert_authority`, `prevent_argument` and
  `took_what_was_not_mine`. They appear on 17 authored events.
- When the actor perceives their own event, `Interpreter.Interpret` gives them that meaning,
  "knew their own intention", without consulting any rule. It becomes their `Experience`.
- Four appraisal rules key on self-meanings. `protect` -> anxiety (`family_safety`).
  `assert_authority` -> anger (`respect`). `took_what_was_not_mine` -> shame (`fairness`) and
  fear (`closeness`).

The 7 wants and the 6 self-meanings are separate vocabularies, and **no data or code maps one
to the other**. The morning's `Happened` passes `null`. The only reason-bearing path from an
act into its actor's memory is therefore built, tested and exercised by authored history, and
unused by everything a person decides to do.

The same under Model A: every choosing of `wait`, `observe`, `comfort`, `search_room` and
`check_pantry` was credited by two or more wants, and 4.00 wants credited each pantry check.
Model B thinned the sums but did not change the map.

**Verdict.** High sharing is not bad in itself, and for walks it is largely legitimate. It is
causally destructive where two conditions meet:
1. The ends are genuinely different (restraint against peace; learning against being seen to
   settle it; suspicion against care).
2. Nothing after the choice keeps the end.

Both conditions hold at `wait`, `search_room`, `check_pantry` and (in principle) `observe`.
The pantry check is also a pure sum: the want said to lead it contributes only 40 % of its
appeal on average.

#### 5. Same action / different reason

The test (`diagnostic.md` §2-3): take acts of the same kind chosen for different leading wants,
and compare everything downstream.

| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **A.** Can the simulation preserve that two people did the same act for different reasons? | **Only until the act starts.** The `Decision` (on `Busy.Decision`) keeps the leading want and every contribution until the act completes. Then it is dropped, except by eating. | code |
| **B.** Can the world event distinguish them? | **No.** 743 of 917 acts that produced an event (81 %) produced exactly the event an act of the same kind produced for a different reason. The other 174 are acts only one want ever led here (watching, comforting). 0 of 918 events carry an intent. | `diagnostic.md` §1-2 |
| **C.** Can others perceive the act and read it differently? | **Yes, but by who they are, never by why it was done.** The same search reads as challenge to Elena and concern to the others, from their roles and beliefs. No rule can read an intent. | §2 pairs; section 2.3 |
| **D.** Can memory preserve the reason? | **No.** The actor's own reading: 0 of 918 are "own intention". Checking the pantry leaves the checker `neutral` whether it was for `find_out` (120), `get_food` (70) or `guard_supplies` (10). Every comforter remembers their own comfort as `support` and feels **grateful** (114 of 114). | §3 |
| **E.** Can a later belief or appraisal depend on the reason? | **No, through perception; yes in principle, through the unused intent channel** (section 2.4). Appraisal and belief nudges read the meaning, and the meaning never carries the reason. | code |
| **F.** Where exactly is it lost? | **Four places**, in order (below). | code |

Where the reason is lost:
1. **`SilentMorning.Begin`, line 288.** The intention formed for the chosen act is kept only
   for a walk.
2. **`SilentMorning.Complete` / `Happened`.** The decision is not passed to the event; `intent`
   is `null`.
3. **`Simulation.Apply(e, causes: null)`.** The event's trace record rests on nothing: 918 of
   918, and 0 of 917 acts have any reading that walks back to the actor's decision or wants.
4. **`Interpreter.Interpret`.** With no intent, the actor reads their own act through the
   witnesses' rules. Nothing in the actor's memory differs from a bystander's.

**Concrete pairs** (`diagnostic.md` §2):
- **Two pantry checks.** Daniel's, led by `find_out` 0.808 and credited by four wants, and
  Elena's, led by `get_food` 0.641. Both produce
  `count_supplies, topic supplies, valence neutral, intent null`. Both checkers and every
  witness keep `neutral` and feel nothing.
- **Two searches.** Daniel's for `find_out`, Mara's for `restore_standing`. Both produce
  `search_belongings, topic missing_can`, both searchers keep `neutral`, and the witnesses'
  readings differ only by who the witnesses are.

#### 6. Different action / same intention

**Yes, partly, and only in deliberation** (`diagnostic.md` §4).

| Want | Leads (of decisions raised) | Acts it led | Distinct acts (targets, destinations) | Standing still among its leads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| find_out | 841 (22.8 %) | search 467, walk 188, check 120, watch 66 | **8** | 0 % |
| look_after | 422 (27.4 %) | comfort 422 | 3 (one per person) | 0 % |
| get_food | 206 (5.6 %) | check 127, walk 79 | 2 | 0 %, and **never eats** |
| guard_supplies | 1,944 (52.7 %) | wait 1,934, check 10 | 2 | **99.5 %** |
| keep_peace | 202 (8.9 %) | wait 202 | 1 | **100 %** |
| avoid_exposure | 67 (3.0 %) | walk 34, wait 33 | 4 | 49.3 % |
| restore_standing | 7 (0.9 %) | search 7 | 1 | 0 % |

**Where the distinction is represented:**
- Proposals give one want several acts with different fits and targeting.
- Targeting and destination distinguish *whom* and *where*.
- The **intention across a walk** is the only representation of a means serving an end
  across two acts. `find_out`'s 188 walks: 173 held on arrival, all 173 then searched, 15
  interrupted. `avoid_exposure`: 31 held, 30 then stood still.
- `get_food`'s 79 walks: 0 held; 75 lapsed as not worth it on arrival, 4 interrupted.

**Where it collapses:**
- The intention is only the want's key. The foreseen end of the walk (`Foresee`, under
  `means: end`) is computed and thrown away (a `foresaw` trace string only).
- Once the search at the end of the walk is done, nothing records that walk and search were
  one pursuit, or what the pursuit was for.
- Execution and memory represent **physical verbs with a target or destination**. Intentions
  exist only as want keys in deliberation and across one walk. Ends exist only as proposal
  fits and one discarded foresight.

one walk, and eating's outcome record, which nothing reads.

#### 8. Standing-still analysis

| Question | Answer (code; `diagnostic.md` §5) |
|---|---|
| Is it an action? | In deliberation, **yes**: `ActionKind.Wait`, always available, 5 minutes, scored like any other. In execution, **no**: `Complete` writes a note and nothing else. |
| Which wants credit it? | `guard_supplies` (`leave_it_alone`, restraint, 0.30), `keep_peace` (`let_it_be`, 0.50), `avoid_exposure` (`stay_out_of_the_way`, alone, 0.45). |
| Why is it free? | **No cost rule names it.** It is the only act without a price, so it scores its appeal while every other act scores appeal minus cost. |
| How often, and why? | 58.8 % of decisions (Model A: 52.9 %). Led by `guard_supplies` 1,934 times, `keep_peace` 202, `avoid_exposure` 33. Never with nothing behind it: `guard_supplies` is raised for everyone, from the belief that supplies are short. Chosen while another act was worth doing in 41.9 % (Model A: 98.6 %). |
| Is the restraint real? | Mostly. Of the 1,934 led by guarding the supplies, 1,781 (92.1 %) had food within reach, so not eating was a real choice; in 153 there was no food in the room. |
| Does it have an intention? | Formed by the deliberator every time (2,169), and dropped by the morning every time. |
| World event, memory? | **None.** 0 events; nobody, including the person, has any record of having refrained, kept the peace or kept out of sight. |
| Uncertainty, or a choice? | A choice: settled by the seed in 20.9 % of cases, like other acts. |

**Verdict: a legitimate behavioural choice with an artifactual representation.** Refraining
from food, letting a thing be and keeping out of sight are real behaviours, and here they are
mostly chosen where they are real. Two things about the representation distort it:
- **The free floor (PLAUSIBLE distortion).** Being the only unpriced act, it wins whenever
  every other act's appeal falls short of its cost. That is why removing the want that "led"
  it changes the act only 20 % of the time under B (Decision Sensitivity §7.1): the label on a
  standing-still is often not what decided it.
- **Three inactions, no consequence (PROVEN).** Restraint, peace-keeping and hiding become one
  act that leaves nothing in the world or in any mind. It is the extreme case of section 5: a
  choice with a reason and no trace at all.

| # | Claim | Label |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Candidate generation is the bottleneck | **FAILED**. 1.9 % of pairs have no candidate (Decision Sensitivity), and hub acts are chosen while other kinds are worth doing. |
| 2 | Motivation-to-deliberation is a bottleneck | **PROVEN for specific wants** (`get_food` is priced out: 78 % outweighed; the urgency ceiling); **PLAUSIBLE** as a general cause |
| 3 | Deliberation/scoring is a bottleneck | **PROVEN in part**. Hub appeal is a sum (the pantry check: 3.42 wants per choosing, leading share 0.40), the band decides 21 % of standing-stills, and standing still is unpriced. |
| 4 | Action representation is a bottleneck | Split in two. **The reason is not represented beyond selection: PROVEN.** **The many-to-one mapping destroys distinctions: PROVEN** where ends differ and nothing keeps them (identical events, 81 %; S1.7's cancellation). As *the* cause of act insensitivity: **PLAUSIBLE**. |
| 5 | Execution / world event is a bottleneck | **PROVEN for meaning.** Events are built from constants per kind; intent is always null; the trace is unlinked. A completion loss for long acts is measured (comfort completes 27 %), but whether it matters is **UNPROVEN**. |
| 6 | Intention / reason is lost after selection | **PROVEN**, at four named places (section 5 F) |
| 7 | Standing still is a default distortion | **PROVEN** that it is unpriced and leaves no trace; **PLAUSIBLE** that its free floor distorts the choice (20 % against 84-88 % sensitivity to the leading want); **FAILED** as "restraint where there is nothing to refrain from" (7.9 % of cases) |
| 8 | Shared hub actions compress psychological distinctions | **PROVEN** that they do, where ends differ; **FAILED** as a problem in itself (walks are legitimate sharing; the destination carries part of the end) |

#### 11. Is the bottleneck an artifact?

Tested against the brief's falsifiers, using existing evidence only.

| If this were true, the hypothesis would weaken | Found |
|---|---|
| Distinct intentions already survive through execution | **No.** 0 of 918 events, 0 of 918 actor readings, 0 of 917 trace paths. Not falsified. |
| Acts are semantically richer than their names | **Partly.** Targets and destinations carry *whom* and *where*, and a search always concerns the missing can. But that is a fixed purpose per kind, not the actor's. Not falsified, and it shows where meaning *is* carried. |
| The pattern appears only because there are no alternatives | **No.** Hubs were chosen while another kind of act was worth doing: pantry check and comfort 100 %, walks 99.7 %, search 63.5 %, standing still 41.9 %. Not falsified. |
| New acts would be needed only because the scenario is impoverished | **Partly true.** That `find_out` can find nothing and `restore_standing` and `keep_peace` have no act that changes what they read is the silent house (nothing to find, nobody speaks), not the architecture. **This part is an artifact** of the scenario. |

**Conclusion.** The **loss of the reason is structural**: it happens at the same four places
whatever the vocabulary, and a larger vocabulary would lose it the same way. The
**selection-level compression is partly an artifact**. It depends on seven kinds of act, the
current proposal fits (`restore_standing` nearly proportional to `find_out`) and a scenario
with no information in it. The Decision Sensitivity report's "action representation" finding
survives, but mainly as the first kind of loss, not the second.

#### 12. What the next experiment should be

Not implemented. The evidence now separates two questions:
1. Would preserving the reason change anything downstream?
2. Would a richer want-to-act mapping make acts respond to motivation?

The first is the cheaper and sharper test, because the channel already exists:
- An event's `intent` already becomes its actor's memory.
- The appraisal rules for self-meanings already exist.
- S1.7's circumstances and the replay instrument already measure downstream change.

**The candidate next experiment:** a switch in the S1.5/S1.6 pattern that lets a morning act's
event carry an intent derived from its leading want. It would be measured against the current
baseline on:
- the actor's memory and appraisal;
- later wants;
- S1.7's 27 circumstances;

with predictions committed first.

**The decision it needs, and it is yours:**
- The 7 wants and the 6 self-meanings do not map onto each other.
- Choosing that mapping is a psychological claim, not a measurement.
- Whether a witness should ever infer an intent is a separate question, and it would need
  rules that do not exist yet.

The second question (the mapping from wants to acts) should wait. Its compression is partly
an artifact of this scenario, and redesigning it now would be tuning the proposals to one
house.

#### 13. Recommendation

**MODIFY.**

- **Keep the direction:** investigate action representation before interpretation strength.
- **Change the focus:** from sharing hub acts to **carrying intentional meaning across the
  action boundary**. That is the loss this audit proves, locates at four lines, and finds
  already half-built.
- Hub compression and standing still's free floor are real. They are secondary, and partly
  products of this scenario.
